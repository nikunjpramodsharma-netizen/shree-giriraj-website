## The short answer

A MHADA layout is redeveloped under Regulation 33(5) of Mumbai's Development Control and Promotion Regulations, 2034, which makes extra floor space available. Before that, the society needs the Board's no objection, a clear lease or conveyance position, and members' consent in the legal form. **The order of these steps matters more than the builder's offer.**

---

## What are the stages of a MHADA redevelopment?

Six stages, in this order, give a society a strong start. Each one is explained below.

| Stage | What the society does | Why it comes at this point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. The lease | Check that the Board's lease is executed and current | The lease gives the society its right to the land |
| 2. The Board's dues | Clear any arrears on the society's account | They surface at the no objection stage |
| 3. Members' transfer files | Regularise any flat that changed hands or passed on a death without the Board's permission | The Board reads the society's file as a whole |
| 4. The regulation | Read the current text of 33(5) and the latest resolution | The figures change by resolution |
| 5. Member consent | Hold the general body meeting in the form the law prescribes | A meeting held on the wrong footing is held again |
| 6. The route | Choose self redevelopment or a developer, then go to the market | Members decide first, on their own terms |

## Why is MHADA redevelopment its own subject?

An ordinary society redevelops on land it owns, under the general regulations. A MHADA society sits on land the Board allotted. The allotment still carries the Board's interest, in a layout the Board planned.

So a MHADA redevelopment has an extra party at the table from the first day. It also has its own regulation in the development code.

That regulation is 33(5) of the Development Control and Promotion Regulations, 2034. It applies to redevelopment of housing schemes of the Maharashtra Housing and Area Development Authority. It is the source of the extra floor space a MHADA layout can use, and of the terms on which it can use it.

Floor space index (FSI) is the amount of floor area you may build compared with the size of the plot. Under 33(5), these figures have been amended by government resolution more than once:

- The floor space index
- The premium payable
- The share of built up area handed to MHADA
- The minimum area per member

We do not quote them here, because any number printed today needs checking against the current text on the day it is used. Read the regulation and the latest resolution before you trust a figure on a builder's presentation.

For a society outside a MHADA layout, read about [redevelopment in a society outside a MHADA layout](/blog/redevelopment-borivali-kandivali).

## Does a MHADA society need conveyance before redevelopment?

The society's position on the land has to be clear. Conveyance is the legal transfer of land to the society. In a MHADA layout, the position is usually a lease from the Board, not an outright transfer.

A society that does not hold the lease cannot sign a development agreement on its own terms. That includes a lease that has not been executed or has lapsed. So the first document to look at is the lease, not the builder's offer.

Our guide to [conveyance and deemed conveyance](/blog/conveyance-and-deemed-conveyance) sets out the general position. For a MHADA society, add the Board's no objection to the list, because a redevelopment under 33(5) runs through the Board.

## What does the Board's no objection cover?

The Board is a party to a 33(5) redevelopment because it is the authority whose scheme is being rebuilt. Its no objection is the point where it confirms three things:

- The society's entitlement
- The dues position
- The terms on which additional floor space is released

Nothing goes to the municipal corporation for approval without it.

This is why each member's paperwork matters to the whole society. A flat transferred without the Board's permission, or a transfer on death never completed, shows up in the society's file when the Board examines it. Putting these right early keeps the file clean.

The [rules on transfer](/blog/can-we-sell-mhada-flat) and [transfer on death](/blog/mhada-transfer-on-death) are not only individual concerns. They are what a clean society file is made of.

## Can a MHADA society redevelop itself?

Yes. MHADA published Guidelines for Self Redevelopment on 26 June 2023. They set out how a society can take on its own redevelopment instead of appointing a developer.

The attraction is clear: the society keeps what a developer would otherwise take as profit. In return, the society carries the financing, the contractor, the approvals and the risk itself.

Read the eligibility conditions, financing arrangements and incentives from the 2023 document before committing. It is a long scanned document, and we do not summarise it here.

Self redevelopment suits a society with:

- A working committee
- Members who agree
- Access to the professionals it will need

It suits a society that has chosen it on its merits, not because a builder's offer looked thin.

## What about member consent?

Redevelopment of a cooperative housing society in Maharashtra needs the members' consent, obtained in the manner the cooperative law and government directions prescribe. That means a general body meeting called for the purpose, with the required majority, minuted and recorded.

Confirm the required percentage and the meeting procedure against the current government direction before you call the meeting. A meeting called on the wrong footing has to be held again.

Whatever the number, the practical advice is the same. Members should decide to redevelop first, on their own terms. Then the society goes to the market. Consent given after the builder has been chosen is consent the members were not really asked for.

## What should a society check before signing anything?

1. **The lease.** Does the society hold one from the Board? Is it executed and current?
2. **The Board's dues.** Clear arrears on the society's account before the no objection stage.
3. **Every member's transfer file.** Regularise any flat that changed hands without permission, and complete any pending transfer on death, before the Board looks at the society as a whole.
4. **The current text of 33(5)** and the latest resolution under it, read by someone who reads regulations, not a brochure.
5. **The consent process,** set up correctly before the vote.
6. **The developer's record of finishing MHADA redevelopments,** not only starting them.

## Common questions

**What is Regulation 33(5)?**
It is the regulation in Mumbai's Development Control and Promotion Regulations, 2034 that governs redevelopment of MHADA housing schemes. It is the source of the extra floor space a MHADA layout can use, and of the terms attached to it. Its figures are amended by resolution, so read them from the current text.

**Does a MHADA society need conveyance before redevelopment?**
Its position on the land has to be clear. In a MHADA layout, that usually means a current lease from the Board. A society without one cannot sign a development agreement on its own terms, and the Board's no objection is needed in any case.

**Can a MHADA society redevelop itself?**
Yes. MHADA's Guidelines for Self Redevelopment, dated 26 June 2023, set out the route. The society keeps the developer's margin and carries the developer's risk.

**Why do individual members' transfer papers matter?**
The Board examines the society's file as a whole. A flat transferred without permission, or a transfer on death never completed, will be found. It is cheaper to regularise before the Board examines the file than during.

**Is the highest offer the best offer?**
Rarely. The offer that gets built is the one that matters, so check the developer's completion record on MHADA schemes.
