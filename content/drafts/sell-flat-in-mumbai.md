## The short answer

To sell a flat in Mumbai, price it against recent registered sales in your building. Gather the title papers a buyer's bank will ask for. Then sign and register the agreement for sale at the sub registrar. The buyer pays stamp duty and registration. **You give the society 15 days' notice and pay its transfer fee, at most 25,500 rupees.**

## How do I price my flat to sell?

**Start from what flats in your pocket ask today, then check what they have actually registered for.** Asking is what similar flats are listed at. The registered price, which appears on Index 2 after a sale, is usually lower.

Then look at registered sales. Every registered sale has an [Index 2](/blog/index-2-property-document), the sub registrar's one page summary with the price. You can search them free on the IGR Maharashtra portal. A price close to recent registrations in your building brings serious buyers quickly.

## What documents do I need to sell my flat?

**A buyer's bank reads your title papers before it lends, so a complete file is what keeps a sale moving.** Gather these before you list the flat.

| Document | Why the buyer asks for it | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your registered agreement for sale, and the earlier ones in the chain | It shows how ownership reached you | Your own file, or a certified copy from the sub registrar |
| Index 2 for each of those registrations | It proves each one was registered | Free search on the IGR Maharashtra portal |
| Share certificate | It shows your membership of the society | The housing society |
| Occupancy certificate | The buyer's lender asks for it | Granted by the BMC; ask the society for a copy |
| Latest property tax and maintenance receipts | They show the dues are clear | The BMC and the society |
| Lender's closure letter and returned originals | They show any old loan is paid off | Your bank, if the flat had a home loan |

Three papers carry most of the weight: the agreement chain with its Index 2, the share certificate, and the occupancy certificate. In Mumbai the BMC grants the occupancy certificate under section 353A of the Mumbai Municipal Corporation Act. Our guide to the [occupancy certificate](/blog/occupancy-certificate) explains what to do if your building does not have one.

The [share certificate guide](/blog/society-share-certificate-transfer-fee) explains what the certificate is. It proves membership of the society, not ownership of the flat.

## Do I need the society's NOC to sell my flat?

**No. The Maharashtra model bye laws say a society's no objection certificate is not required to transfer a flat.** What the society does need is notice and its fees.

- **Notice:** you give the society 15 days' notice of the intended transfer, under rule 24 of the Maharashtra Cooperative Societies Rules.
- **Fees:** the seller pays a 500 rupee transfer fee and a premium capped at 25,000 rupees in a municipal corporation area. The buyer pays a 100 rupee entrance fee.
- **NOC on request:** if you or the buyer's bank ask for an NOC, the committee considers it within one month.

Many buyers' banks still like to see a society letter on file. Our guide to the [society NOC for the sale of a flat](/blog/society-noc-for-sale-of-flat-mumbai) has a model request letter.

## How does a flat sale in Mumbai work, step by step?

**A resale flat in Mumbai changes hands through one registered agreement for sale.** These are the steps in the order they happen, from the price to the keys.

1. **Fix the asking price** from your pocket's rate and the registered sales near your building.
2. **Gather the file:** the agreement chain, Index 2, share certificate, occupancy certificate and dues receipts.
3. **Show the flat** to buyers who can complete, with their budget and loan position known.
4. **Agree the terms in writing:** the price, the token, the payment dates, the handover date and who pays which society charge.
5. **Let the buyer's bank check the title** and sanction the loan.
6. **Sign and register the agreement for sale** at the sub registrar's office.
7. **Transfer the shares** in the society to the buyer, after your 15 days' notice and the fees.
8. **Hand over the keys** once the full price has reached you.

At step 6 the buyer pays the stamp duty. It is 6 percent for a man, or for a man and a woman buying jointly. Where women are the only buyers it is 5 percent. Registration is 1 percent, capped at 30,000 rupees.

Under section 32A of the Registration Act, the sub registrar takes photographs and fingerprints of each buyer and seller. For a Mumbai resale with possession, the registered agreement is treated as the conveyance itself, as our page on [what a sale deed contains](/blog/what-a-sale-deed-contains) explains.

## Can I sell a flat that still has a home loan?

**Yes. Your bank holds the original papers until the loan is paid off, so the sale is planned around clearing it.** Once the loan is closed, your bank issues a closure letter and returns the originals, and both go into the buyer's file. Tell your bank early that you are selling, and ask for the outstanding amount in writing. We then set the payment dates in the agreement around it.

## Common questions

**Who pays stamp duty when I sell my flat in Mumbai?**
The buyer pays stamp duty and the registration fee. In Mumbai the duty is 6 percent for a man, or a man and a woman buying jointly. Where women are the only buyers it is 5 percent. Registration is 1 percent, capped at 30,000 rupees.

**Do I need a society NOC to sell my flat in Mumbai?**
No. The Maharashtra model bye laws say an NOC is not required to transfer a flat. You give the society 15 days' notice and pay the transfer fee. If you or a bank ask for an NOC, the committee considers it within one month.

**How much is the society transfer fee when I sell my flat?**
At most 25,500 rupees from the seller in Mumbai: a 500 rupee transfer fee plus a premium capped at 25,000 rupees. The buyer pays a 100 rupee entrance fee. Buyer and seller can agree to share the cost and should write it into the agreement.

**What documents are needed to sell a flat in Mumbai?**
Start with the registered agreement for sale and the earlier agreements in the chain, each with its Index 2. Add the share certificate, the occupancy certificate, and the latest property tax and maintenance receipts. If the flat had a home loan, add the lender's closure letter.

**How do I find out what flats in my building sold for?**
Search the registered sales on the IGR Maharashtra portal. Each sale has an Index 2, which shows the parties, the property and the price. Send us the society name and we send recent registered sales near your building, free.

**How do I calculate the value of my flat?**
Multiply the carpet area by the current asking rate for your pocket, then compare with recent registered sales in the building. As of November 2026, Borivali West resale flats asked a median of 36,228 rupees per sq ft of carpet (Mumbai Property Exchange, week ending 15 November). The valuation calculator on this site does the first part for you.

**Can I sell my MHADA flat?**
Yes, once five years have passed since allotment. You need the written permission of the Board and the society. The buyer must be in the scheme's income group, and the Board's dues must be paid. We prepare that file for MHADA flats anywhere in Mumbai.
