## The short answer

An occupancy certificate is issued by the local authority confirming a building is complete in line with the approved plans and is fit to live in. **Without one, a building is not legally cleared for occupation**, and that can affect water and electricity connections, resale, and your ability to get a loan against the flat.

---

## Why builders hand over without one

Possession and legal occupation are two different events. A builder under pressure to show delivery will offer possession before the certificate has come through, sometimes long before.

Taking those keys feels like progress. It moves your risk from the builder to you.

---

## What it affects

- **Utilities.** Permanent water and electricity connections can depend on it [ VERIFY for Mumbai specifically ]
- **Resale.** A future buyer's lender will ask, and some will not lend without it
- **Loan against property.** Same problem, later
- **Regularisation risk.** If the building deviates from approved plans, the deviation is now your problem

---

## What to ask before possession

1. Has the OC been issued, or applied for?
2. If applied for, when, and what is outstanding?
3. Is it full or part occupancy? [ VERIFY: whether part OC is a thing in Mumbai and what it means ]
4. Can I see it?

**Ask to see the document, not to be told it exists.**
