## The short answer

Buying a MHADA flat in resale means buying an allotment with conditions. Before money moves, check six things: the allottee, the five years, the income group, both permissions, the charges and the dues. **A sale without the Board's permission can be cancelled.**

---

| # | Check | What to ask for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The seller is the Board's allottee | Allotment letter and latest Board receipt in the seller's name |
| 2 | Five years have passed from allotment | The date on the allotment letter |
| 3 | You fall in the scheme's income group | Your income proof and affidavits |
| 4 | Both permissions will be obtained | A sale agreement that depends on the Board's and the society's written permission |
| 5 | You know the transfer cost | The Board's charge and any unearned increase, in writing, with who pays |
| 6 | Dues are clear | The Board's latest receipt and the society's no dues certificate |

## What does a careful buyer check first?

The Estate Management, Sale, Transfer and Exchange of Tenements Regulations, 1981 put the consequences of an unpermitted transfer on the buyer. A tenement is the flat or unit the Board has let to the allottee.

If the Board declares a transfer invalid, the buyer is treated as an unauthorised occupant. The Board refunds the original allottee's payment to the allottee, without interest. It does not refund the buyer's purchase price.

So these checks are what make the flat truly yours. Each one is simple to do.

---

## Is it safe to buy a MHADA flat in resale?

It is safe when the transfer is done the way the Regulations require. Six things decide that.

### 1. The seller is the Board's allottee

Ask for the [allotment letter](/blog/mhada-allotment-letter) and the latest rent or maintenance receipt from the Board. The name on both should be the seller's.

If the seller inherited the flat, the Board should already have granted the transfer to them. If not, the seller cannot yet sell. The [transfer on death](/blog/mhada-transfer-on-death) comes first.

### 2. Five years have passed from allotment

The Regulations allow the society to permit a transfer once five years have passed from the date of allotment. Count from the allotment letter, not from when the seller moved in.

Read the letter's own conditions too. Some schemes set a longer period than five years.

### 3. You fall in the scheme's income group

For Economically Weaker Section, Low Income Group and Middle Income Group schemes, the buyer must fall in one of those groups. For High Income Group schemes, the buyer must fall in that group.

The Board's document list asks for proof of income and an income affidavit. It also asks for an affidavit that you and your immediate family hold no other land or tenement in Mumbai city and suburbs. You can make these declarations truthfully if they apply to you, and the transfer can then be granted.

### 4. Both permissions will be obtained

The Board's previous permission in writing, and the society's, are both needed. Make the agreement depend on both. Then your money moves only when the permissions are in hand.

### 5. You know what the transfer will cost

The Mumbai Board's published schedule for transfer of tenancy rights is:

| Income group | Rate | Maximum charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LIG | 45 rupees per sq ft | 15,000 rupees |
| MIG | 55 rupees per sq ft | 25,000 rupees |
| HIG | 60 rupees per sq ft | 35,000 rupees |

These are as published on mhada.gov.in, checked on 9 September 2026.

The Board may also make its permission conditional on a share of the unearned increase. That share is up to fifty percent, at its discretion. The unearned increase is the rise in the flat's value since allotment.

Agree in writing who pays each charge before you agree the price. Our guide to the [MHADA transfer process and documents](/blog/mhada-flat-transfer) shows the charge for flats of different sizes and the Board's own checklist.

### 6. The society's dues and the Board's dues are clear

The Board's list asks for the latest receipt showing no arrears. Ask for it, and for the society's no dues certificate, before the agreement.

## Do the ordinary checks still apply?

Yes. A MHADA flat is still a flat. The MHADA layer sits on top of the usual checks. It does not replace them.

- Get the [encumbrance certificate](/blog/encumbrance-certificate), the record of loans or claims on the property.
- Check the society's share certificate and the position on conveyance, the legal transfer of land to the society.
- Measure the carpet area rather than accepting the built up figure.
- Register the agreement for sale.

## Can I get a home loan on a MHADA resale flat?

Lenders do finance MHADA flats. They ask for the Board's no objection certificate for mortgage before sanction.

That NOC is its own application to the Estate Manager, on the Mumbai Board's Form 4. The applicant is the person on the Board's record. So the Board's forms point to the transfer coming first.

Build the time into your plan. A sanction that is waiting on a Board NOC is one the seller may not wait for.

Lenders' policies on MHADA flats differ, including whether they want the transfer completed before disbursement. Ask your lender in writing before relying on the loan.

The Board's other services and their time limits are in [MHADA NOC online](/blog/mhada-noc-online). Our guide to a [home loan on a MHADA flat](/blog/home-loan-on-mhada-flat) has the Mumbai Board's Form 4 checklist and the 12 working day limit.

## Common questions

**Is it safe to buy a MHADA flat in resale?**
Yes, if the transfer is done under the Regulations. The seller is the Board's allottee, five years have passed from allotment, and you fall in the scheme's income group. Both the Board's and the society's written permission come before the sale goes unconditional. Without those, the Board can cancel the transfer.

**Do I need to be in a particular income group to buy a MHADA flat?**
Yes. The Regulations require the buyer to fall in the income group of the scheme. The Board's list asks for proof and an affidavit. It also asks for an affidavit that you hold no other land or tenement in Mumbai.

**Can I get a home loan on a MHADA resale flat?**
Generally yes, with the Board's no objection certificate for mortgage, which is a separate application. Confirm your lender's requirements in writing before you depend on the loan.

**Who pays the MHADA transfer charges?**
Whoever the agreement says. The Board's schedule and any unearned increase are real costs. The agreement should name the paying party for each before the price is fixed.

**What if the seller says nobody bothers with the permission?**
The Regulations allow the Board to cancel exactly that kind of transfer. The way forward is to do it properly, with both permissions in hand, and we are glad to help you through it.
