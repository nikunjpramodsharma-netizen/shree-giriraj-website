## What is a MHADA allotment letter?

A MHADA allotment letter records that MHADA, the Maharashtra Housing and Area Development Authority, has allotted a particular flat to you. In Mumbai, the allotment is made by MHADA's Mumbai Housing and Area Development Board (the Mumbai Board). Its Marathi forms call the letter the वितरण पत्र (vitaran patra).

The Mumbai Board's transfer, regularisation, mortgage NOC and sale permission checklists all list it. Each asks for a copy of the allotment letter. If the flat has been transferred, each also asks for the stamp duty receipts for every change of hands.

## What does a MHADA offer letter say? Format and what each line means

MHADA's published procedure for allotment of residential tenements sets out what the offer to an eligible allottee contains. A tenement is the flat or unit the Board lets to the allottee. Here is each line and what it means for you:

| Line in the offer letter | What it means | Where it matters later |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The flat number | Which flat has been allotted to you | Quote it on every Board form and receipt |
| The area | The size of the flat | Check it against the flat you are shown |
| The cost | The amount MHADA asks for the flat | Your payment receipts go in the file |
| The time limit to deposit the amount | The date by which money is due; the first 10 percent is due within 30 to 45 days | Keeps your allotment on track |
| The payment account | The virtual account the offer letter gives for payment, full or part | Pay only to this account and keep the receipts |
| Possession | Handed over after full payment | The step that follows full payment |

MHADA's procedure says possession of the flat is handed over only after full payment. Today's offer letter also sets out the payment, as the stages below show.

## What are the MHADA offer letter stages?

A MHADA lottery winner receives a Conditional Provisional Offer Letter, then a Final one. Possession follows full payment.

| Stage | What MHADA issues or asks | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| After the draw results are announced | Conditional Provisional Offer Letter (POL), in the MHADA IHLMS 2.0 app or by email | MHADA lottery FAQ |
| After MHADA verifies your documents | Final Provisional Offer Letter; the original is collected at the MHADA office with the original documents the Conditional POL lists | MHADA lottery FAQ |
| As the offer letter sets out | Payment, full or part, to the virtual account it gives; the first 10 percent within 30 to 45 days | MHADA lottery FAQ |
| After full payment | Possession of the flat | MHADA's published allotment procedure |

MHADA's lottery FAQ calls the winner's letter the Provisional Offer Letter. It does not say whether this is the same as the allotment letter (vitaran patra) the Board's later forms ask for. So keep the original POL and every later letter the Board issues, together with your payment receipts.

## How do you get and download your MHADA allotment letter?

The letter MHADA lets lottery winners download is the Provisional Offer Letter. MHADA's sources do not say the allotment letter itself can be downloaded. The lottery FAQ describes three steps:

1. **Download the Conditional POL** from your login in the MHADA IHLMS 2.0 mobile application, or from the email MHADA sends, once results are announced.
2. **Complete document verification.** MHADA issues the Final POL only after its officials verify your documents.
3. **Collect the original** at the MHADA office, bringing the original documents the Conditional POL lists.

### Where does MHADA lottery registration fit?

Lottery registration comes before any letter. It is done in one of two places:

- MHADA's official lottery website, housing.mhada.gov.in
- The "MHADA Housing Lottery System" app, from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store

The whole application is online, with no hard copy to submit.

## Why does the allotment letter matter later?

The allotment letter travels with the flat. The Board's checklists for the jobs below all list it.

| What you want to do | Board form | Who applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer the flat to a family member or an heir | Form 1 | The person taking the flat by registered gift deed, or the heir |
| Regularise an earlier purchase | The regularisation form published with Form 1 | The person who bought by a registered purchase deed |
| Take a loan against the flat | Form 4 | The person the flat is allotted or regularised to |
| Sell the flat | Form 5 (sale permission) | The person the flat is allotted or regularised to |

In each checklist, the item asks for a certified copy of the allotment letter. If the flat has been transferred, it also asks for certified copies of the stamp duty receipts for every change.

Once the flat is yours, your own mortgage NOC and sale permission applications list the letter again. When you sell, hand it over with the file.

## MHADA allotment letter lost? How to get a certified copy

If your allotment letter is lost, the Mumbai Board's route is Form 9. It is an application for certified copies of the papers in your flat's file. On it, you state that the papers are lost and are needed for court, personal or similar purposes. You agree to pay the fee and list the documents you want copied.

Looking for a duplicate allotment letter from MHADA? None of the Mumbai Board's nine published forms (Forms 1 to 9) is a duplicate service. For lost papers, the route is this certified copy from your flat's file, on Form 9.

Form 9's checklist asks for two things:

1. **Certified copies of the receipts** for all arrears paid and the current month.
2. **The list of documents** you want copied, such as the allotment letter.

A competent authority attests the attached papers. The officer may ask for more papers where a case needs them.

The application goes to the Estate Manager, the Mumbai Board officer who handles the flats in your division. Certified copies are a notified service. The limit is 8 office working days under MHADA's notification of 13 July 2015.

A bank or buyer decides for itself whether to accept a certified copy in place of the original, so ask them early. Our guide to the [MHADA office in Bandra](/blog/mhada-office-bandra) lists which Estate Manager handles each area.

## Common questions

**What is a MHADA allotment letter?**
It is the letter recording that MHADA has allotted a particular flat to you. The Mumbai Board's Marathi forms call it the वितरण पत्र (vitaran patra). The Board's transfer, regularisation, mortgage NOC and sale permission checklists all list it. If the flat has changed hands, they also ask for the stamp duty receipts for every change.

**How do I download my MHADA allotment letter?**
MHADA's published download is for the Provisional Offer Letter, the first letter a lottery winner receives. Its sources do not say the allotment letter itself can be downloaded. Winners download the POL from the MHADA IHLMS 2.0 app or from MHADA's email. The Conditional POL comes once results are announced. The Final POL follows after MHADA verifies your documents. For the original copy, visit the MHADA office with your original documents.

**Is the Provisional Offer Letter the same as the allotment letter?**
MHADA's sources do not say so. The lottery FAQ calls the winner's letter the Provisional Offer Letter. The Mumbai Board's later forms ask for the allotment letter. Keep the original POL and every later letter, with your payment receipts, so whichever paper a form asks for is on hand.

**How do I get a duplicate allotment letter from MHADA?**
None of the Mumbai Board's nine published forms is a duplicate service. Its route for lost papers is Form 9, an application for certified copies of the papers in your flat's file, stating the papers are lost. The checklist asks for your dues receipts and a list of the documents you want. It is a notified service with a limit of 8 office working days.

**What is the MHADA allotment letter called in Marathi?**
The Mumbai Board's Marathi forms call it the वितरण पत्र (vitaran patra). Its checklists ask for a certified copy of the allotment letter. If the flat has changed hands, they also ask for the stamp duty receipts for every change.

**Is the MHADA lottery deposit refundable?**
Yes, in most cases. MHADA's lottery FAQ says applicants who are not selected get the full earnest money deposit back. So do winners who surrender before the Final POL is issued. A winner who surrenders after the Final POL has 10 percent of the deposit deducted, and the rest is refunded.

**Where do I register for the MHADA lottery?**
On MHADA's official lottery website, housing.mhada.gov.in, or in the MHADA Housing Lottery System app on the Google Play Store or Apple App Store. The application is fully online. MHADA's lottery FAQ says no hard copy of the form needs to be submitted.

**Is it safe to buy a MHADA flat?**
It can be, when the Board's papers are in order. The Board's own forms point to these papers:

- The allotment letter
- The stamp duty receipts for every change, if the flat has changed hands
- Receipts for all arrears and the current month
- The society's NOC
- The seller's declaration of no loan and no court case

**Can I sell a MHADA lottery flat?**
Yes, with the written permission of the Mumbai Board and the society. Five years must have passed from allotment under the 1981 Regulations. The buyer's income group must fit, the Board's dues must be paid, and any premium the Board sets applies. The seller's sale permission form lists the allotment letter.
