## What is a MHADA allotment letter?

A MHADA allotment letter is the letter recording that a particular flat has been allotted to you by MHADA, the Maharashtra Housing and Area Development Authority. In Mumbai the allotment is made by MHADA's Mumbai Housing and Area Development Board (the Mumbai Board), whose Marathi forms call it the वितरण पत्र (vitaran patra).

The Mumbai Board's transfer, regularisation, mortgage NOC and sale permission checklists all list it. Each asks for a copy of the allotment letter and, if the flat has been transferred, of the stamp duty receipts for every change of hands.

### What a MHADA offer letter contains

MHADA's published procedure for allotment of residential tenements sets out what the offer to an eligible allottee contains:

- **The flat number**
- **The area**
- **The cost**
- **The time limit to deposit the amount**

MHADA's allotment procedure also says possession of the flat is handed over only after full payment. Today's offer letter also sets out the payment, as the stages below show.

## MHADA offer letter stages: what comes when

A MHADA lottery winner receives a Conditional Provisional Offer Letter, then a Final one, and gets possession after full payment:

| Stage | What MHADA issues or asks | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| After the draw results are announced | Conditional Provisional Offer Letter (POL), in the MHADA IHLMS 2.0 app or by email | MHADA lottery FAQ |
| After MHADA verifies your documents | Final Provisional Offer Letter; the original is collected at the MHADA office with the original documents the Conditional POL lists | MHADA lottery FAQ |
| As the offer letter sets out | Payment, full or part, to the virtual account it gives; the first 10 percent within 30 to 45 days | MHADA lottery FAQ |
| After full payment | Possession of the flat | MHADA's published allotment procedure |

MHADA's lottery FAQ calls the winner's letter the Provisional Offer Letter. It does not say whether this is the वितरण पत्र that the Board's later forms ask for, so keep the original POL and every later letter the Board issues, together with your payment receipts.

## How to get and download your MHADA allotment letter

The letter MHADA lets lottery winners download is the Provisional Offer Letter; MHADA's sources do not say the allotment letter itself can be downloaded. MHADA's lottery FAQ describes three steps:

1. **Download the Conditional POL** from your login in the MHADA IHLMS 2.0 mobile application, or from the email MHADA sends, once results are announced.
2. **Complete document verification.** MHADA issues the Final POL only after its officials verify your documents.
3. **Collect the original** at the MHADA office, bringing the original documents the Conditional POL lists.

### Where MHADA lottery registration fits

MHADA lottery registration comes before any letter. It is done on MHADA's official lottery website, housing.mhada.gov.in, or in the "MHADA Housing Lottery System" app from the Google Play Store or Apple App Store, and the whole application is online, with no hard copy to submit.

## Why the allotment letter matters later

The allotment letter matters later because the Mumbai Board's transfer, regularisation, mortgage NOC and sale permission checklists all list it:

| What you want to do | Board form | Who applies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer the flat to a family member or an heir | Form 1 | The person taking the flat by registered gift deed, or the heir |
| Regularise an earlier purchase | The regularisation form published with Form 1 | The person who bought by a registered purchase deed |
| Take a loan against the flat | Form 4 | The person the flat is allotted or regularised to |
| Sell the flat | Form 5 (sale permission) | The person the flat is allotted or regularised to |

In each checklist the item reads "वितरण पत्र / तसेच हस्तांतरण झाले असल्यास सर्व बदलांवर मुद्रांक शुल्क भरल्याच्या पावतीची प्रमाणित प्रत": a certified copy of the allotment letter and, if the flat has been transferred, of the stamp duty receipts for every change.

The allotment letter travels with the flat. Once the flat is yours, your own mortgage NOC and sale permission applications list it again. When you sell, hand it over with the file. ## MHADA allotment letter lost? How to get a certified copy

If your MHADA allotment letter is lost, the Mumbai Board's route is Form 9, an application for certified copies of the papers in your flat's file. The applicant states that the flat's papers have been lost and are needed for court, personal or similar purposes. They agree to pay the fee and ask for certified copies of the documents named in an attached list.

Looking for a duplicate allotment letter from MHADA? None of the Mumbai Board's nine published forms (Forms 1 to 9) is a duplicate service. For lost papers its route is this certified copy from your flat's file, applied for on Form 9.

Form 9's checklist asks for two things:

1. **Certified copies of the receipts** for all arrears paid and the current month.
2. **The list of documents** you want copied, such as the allotment letter.

The applicant has the attached papers attested by a competent authority, and the officer may ask for further papers where a case needs them. The application goes to the Estate Manager, the Mumbai Board officer who handles the flats in your division. Certified copies of the papers in a file are a notified service, with a limit of 8 office working days under MHADA's notification of 13 July 2015.

Whether a bank or a buyer accepts a certified copy in place of the original is their decision, so ask them early. Our guide to the [MHADA office in Bandra](/blog/mhada-office-bandra) lists which Estate Manager handles each area.

## Common questions

**What is a MHADA allotment letter?**
It is the letter recording that a particular flat has been allotted to you by MHADA, called the वितरण पत्र (vitaran patra) in the Mumbai Board's Marathi forms. The Board's transfer, regularisation, mortgage NOC and sale permission checklists all list it, with the stamp duty receipts for every change if the flat has been transferred.

**How do I download my MHADA allotment letter?**
MHADA's published download is for the Provisional Offer Letter, the first letter a lottery winner receives; its sources do not say the allotment letter itself can be downloaded. Lottery winners download the POL from the MHADA IHLMS 2.0 mobile application or from MHADA's email. The Conditional POL comes once results are announced, and the Final POL after MHADA verifies your documents. For the original copy, MHADA's lottery FAQ says you must visit the MHADA office with your original documents.

**Is the Provisional Offer Letter the same as the allotment letter?**
MHADA's sources do not say so. The lottery FAQ calls the winner's letter the Provisional Offer Letter, while the Mumbai Board's later forms ask for the वितरण पत्र, the allotment letter. Keep the original POL and every later letter the Board issues, with your payment receipts, so whichever paper a form asks for is on hand.

**How do I get a duplicate allotment letter from MHADA?**
None of the Mumbai Board's nine published forms is a duplicate service. Its route for lost papers is Form 9, an application for certified copies of the papers in your flat's file, stating the papers are lost. The checklist asks for your dues receipts and a list of the documents you want, and it is a notified service with a limit of 8 office working days.

**What is the MHADA allotment letter called in Marathi?**
The Mumbai Board's Marathi forms call it the वितरण पत्र (vitaran patra). The checklists usually list it with the stamp duty receipts, as a certified copy of the allotment letter and, if the flat has been transferred, of the stamp duty receipts for every change.

**Is the MHADA lottery deposit refundable?**
Yes, in most cases. MHADA's lottery FAQ says applicants who are not selected get the full earnest money deposit back, and so do winners who surrender before the Final POL is issued. A winner who surrenders after the Final POL has 10 percent of the deposit deducted, and the rest is refunded.

**Where do I register for the MHADA lottery?**
On MHADA's official lottery website, housing.mhada.gov.in, or in the MHADA Housing Lottery System app on the Google Play Store or Apple App Store. The application is fully online; MHADA's lottery FAQ says no hard copy of the form needs to be submitted.

**Is it safe to buy a MHADA flat?**
It can be, when the Board's papers are in order. The Board's own forms point to the allotment letter, the stamp duty receipts for every change if the flat has changed hands, receipts for all arrears and the current month, the society's NOC, and the seller's declaration of no loan and no court case. **Can I sell a MHADA lottery flat?**
Yes, with the written permission of the Mumbai Board and the society, once five years have passed from allotment under the 1981 Regulations, subject to the buyer's income group, the Board's dues being paid and any premium the Board sets. The seller's sale permission form lists the allotment letter. Our guide on [whether you can sell a MHADA flat](/blog/can-we-sell-mhada-flat) has the full rules.
