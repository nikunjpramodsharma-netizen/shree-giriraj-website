## The short answer

When a MHADA allottee dies, the flat can pass to a legal heir. Section 24 of the Maharashtra Housing and Area Development Act, 1976 lists them. The list covers the spouse, an unmarried child, a married son and family, a widowed daughter and her unmarried children, and the parents. **Apply to the Estate Manager with the death and heirship certificates.**

---

## Who can inherit a MHADA flat?

The Mumbai Board's document list for transfer of tenancy rights reproduces the list from section 24 of the Act. Tenancy rights can pass on death to these legal heirs of the original tenement holder. A tenement is the flat or unit the Board has let to the allottee.

- The husband or wife
- An unmarried son or daughter
- A married son and his family members
- A widowed daughter and her unmarried children
- The father and mother

This list is narrower than the ordinary law of succession, so read it closely. A married daughter is not on it in her own right. A brother or a nephew is not on it at all.

If the person living in the flat and paying for it is not on the list, take advice before you apply. A short conversation first saves a refused application later.

---

## What do the Regulations say?

The 1981 Regulations cover this case. If an allottee dies, the Chief Officer may permit the tenement to move to another member of the allottee's family. That member must undertake to abide by the Regulations.

It is a permission granted on application. It is not automatic. Until it is granted, the Board's record still shows the deceased as the allottee. Until then, the flat cannot be sold, let or mortgaged in anyone else's name.

That is why this transfer comes before every other piece of MHADA paperwork. An heir who wants to sell or let the flat has to become the allottee first.

---

## What does the Estate Manager ask for?

The Mumbai Board publishes the list. For a transfer on death, these nine items matter most:

| # | Document | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The application, in the prescribed format, from the Estate Manager's office at Griha Nirman Bhavan, Bandra East | Your request |
| 2 | The original death certificate of the tenement holder | The death |
| 3 | A heirship certificate from the competent authority | Your relationship to the tenement holder |
| 4 | The cooperative society's no objection certificate, or an affidavit that no society has been formed | The society agrees |
| 5 | An affidavit that you, your spouse and unmarried children hold no other land or tenement in Mumbai city and suburbs | No other property |
| 6 | Proof of income: an employer's certificate if in service, otherwise an income affidavit and a copy of the income tax return | Your income group |
| 7 | Two attested photographs of the applicant | Identity |
| 8 | The latest rent receipt showing no arrears | Dues are clear |
| 9 | The transfer charges, paid by demand draft or pay order, with the receipt | Charges paid |

The list says the application with documents goes to the Estate Manager within fifteen days.

If more than one heir is eligible, the Board expects the others' consent in writing before it transfers to one of them. This is where these applications most often stall. A family conversation before the file goes in keeps things moving.

## Are transfer charges payable on a transfer to an heir?

The Board's document list sets the transfer charges for transfer of tenancy rights generally. It does not carve out an exemption for heirs. The charges are:

| Income group | Rate | Maximum charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low Income Group | 45 rupees per sq ft | 15,000 rupees |
| Middle Income Group | 55 rupees per sq ft | 25,000 rupees |
| High Income Group | 60 rupees per sq ft | 35,000 rupees |

These were published on mhada.gov.in and checked on 9 September 2026. Our guide to the [MHADA flat transfer process](/blog/mhada-flat-transfer) has the Board's Form 1 checklist, including the papers for a transfer after a death.

The published list does not say whether the Board applies the full schedule, a reduced charge or no charge to an heir. Practice has varied. Confirm the figure for your scheme at the Estate Manager's office.

## Is a heirship certificate needed for MHADA transfer?

Yes, per the Board's list. A heirship certificate is a document from the competent authority that establishes the applicant's relationship to the deceased allottee. The Board asks for it alongside the death certificate.

The court issues it, and it takes time, so it is the first thing to start.

A nomination with the society is not a substitute. It tells the society who to deal with. It does not make the nominee the Board's allottee, and it does not decide inheritance between heirs.

## Common questions

**Who can inherit a MHADA flat?**
The heirs listed in section 24 of the Act are:

- The spouse
- An unmarried son or daughter
- A married son and his family
- A widowed daughter and her unmarried children
- The parents

Anyone outside that list should take advice before applying.

**Is a heirship certificate needed for MHADA transfer?**
Yes. The Mumbai Board's document list asks for a heirship certificate from the competent authority, alongside the original death certificate. Start it first, because it takes the longest.

**Are transfer charges payable on a transfer to an heir?**
The published schedule applies to transfer of tenancy rights generally and does not exempt heirs on its face. Confirm the charge for your scheme at the Estate Manager's office.

**Can the heir sell the flat straight after the transfer?**
Selling is a separate application with its own conditions. It needs the Board's and society's permission, the buyer's income group to fit, and five years from allotment. Read [can we sell a MHADA flat](/blog/can-we-sell-mhada-flat) before pricing it. A buyer should also read [how to buy a MHADA flat in resale](/blog/how-to-buy-mhada-flat-in-resale). The Board's other permissions are listed in [MHADA NOC online](/blog/mhada-noc-online).

**What if the family was already living in the flat?**
Living there does not change the record. Until the transfer is granted, the Board's allottee is the person who has died. The family should apply rather than wait.
