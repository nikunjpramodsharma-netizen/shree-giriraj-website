## The short answer

When a MHADA allottee dies, the tenancy can be transferred to a legal heir listed in section 24 of the Maharashtra Housing and Area Development Act, 1976: the husband or wife, an unmarried son or daughter, a married son and his family, a widowed daughter and her unmarried children, or the parents. **The application goes to the Estate Manager with the death certificate and a heirship certificate.**

---

## Who can inherit a MHADA flat?

The Mumbai Board's document list for transfer of tenancy rights reproduces the list from section 24 of the Act. Transfer of tenancy rights on death is available to these legal heirs of the original tenement holder:

- the husband or wife
- an unmarried son or daughter
- a married son and his family members
- a widowed daughter and her unmarried children
- the father and mother

That is a narrower list than the ordinary law of succession, and it is worth reading closely. A married daughter is not on it in her own right. A brother or a nephew is not on it at all. Where the person who has been living in the flat and paying for it is not on the list, the transfer is not a formality, and the family should take advice before the application goes in rather than after it is refused.

---

## What the Regulations say

The 1981 Regulations provide that if an allottee dies, the tenement may be permitted by the Chief Officer to be transferred in the name of another member of the allottee's family, provided that member undertakes to abide by the Regulations. It is a permission, granted on application, not an automatic devolution. Until it is granted, the Board's record still shows the deceased as the allottee, and nothing else can be done with the flat: it cannot be sold, let, or mortgaged in anyone else's name.

That is why this transfer comes before every other piece of MHADA paperwork a family might need. A heir who wants to sell or let the flat has to become the allottee first.

## The file the Estate Manager wants

The Mumbai Board publishes the list. For a transfer on death, the items that matter most are these:

1. **The application** in the prescribed format, available from the Estate Manager's office at Griha Nirman Bhavan, Bandra East
2. **The original death certificate** of the tenement holder
3. **A heirship certificate** from the competent authority, showing the applicant's relationship to the tenement holder
4. **The no objection certificate from the cooperative society**, or an affidavit that no society has been formed
5. **An affidavit** that the applicant, spouse and unmarried children hold no other land or tenement in Mumbai city and suburbs
6. **Proof of income**: an employer's certificate if in service, otherwise an income affidavit and a copy of the income tax return
7. **Two attested photographs** of the applicant
8. **The latest rent receipt** showing no arrears
9. **The transfer charges**, paid by demand draft or pay order and receipted

The list says the application with documents is to be submitted to the Estate Manager within fifteen days. Where there is more than one eligible heir, the Board will expect the others' consent in writing before it transfers to one of them. This is where these applications most often stall, and it is a family conversation to have before the file goes in.

## Are transfer charges payable on a transfer to an heir?

The Board's document list sets the transfer charges as a general item for transfer of tenancy rights and does not carve out an exemption for heirs: 45 rupees per square foot for Low Income Group capped at 15,000 rupees, 55 for Middle Income Group capped at 25,000, and 60 for High Income Group capped at 35,000, as published on mhada.gov.in and checked on 9 September 2026.

Whether the Mumbai Board applies the full schedule, a reduced charge or no charge to a transfer to a legal heir in practice should be confirmed at the Estate Manager's office for the specific scheme. The published list does not distinguish, and practice has varied.

---

## Is a heirship certificate needed for MHADA transfer?

Yes, per the Board's list. A heirship certificate from the competent authority is the document that establishes the applicant's relationship to the deceased allottee, and the Board asks for it alongside the death certificate. It is issued by the court, and obtaining it takes time, so it is the first thing to start.

A nomination with the society is not a substitute. A society nomination tells the society who to deal with; it does not make the nominee the Board's allottee, and it does not decide inheritance between heirs.

## Common questions

**Who can inherit a MHADA flat?**
The heirs listed in section 24 of the Act: the spouse, an unmarried son or daughter, a married son and his family, a widowed daughter and her unmarried children, and the parents. Anyone outside that list needs advice before applying.

**Is a heirship certificate needed for MHADA transfer?**
Yes. The Mumbai Board's document list asks for a heirship certificate from the competent authority alongside the original death certificate. Start it first, because it takes the longest.

**Are transfer charges payable on a transfer to an heir?**
The published schedule applies to transfer of tenancy rights generally and does not exempt heirs on its face. Confirm the charge for your scheme at the Estate Manager's office.

**Can the heir sell the flat straight after the transfer?**
Selling is a separate application with its own conditions: the Board's and society's permission, the buyer's income group, and the five year rule counted from allotment. Read [can we sell a MHADA flat](/blog/can-we-sell-mhada-flat) before pricing it.

**What if the family was already living in the flat?**
Living there does not change the record. Until the transfer is granted, the Board's allottee is the person who has died, and the family should apply rather than wait.
