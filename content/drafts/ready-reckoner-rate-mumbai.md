## The short answer

The ready reckoner rate is the government's own value for property in each zone, published yearly as the Annual Statement of Rates. **Stamp duty is charged on the agreed price or the reckoner value, whichever is higher.** For 2026 to 2027, most residential zones in Borivali, Kandivali and Malad run from about 1.0 to 1.9 lakh rupees per sq m.

## Why check the reckoner before you fix a price

Most buyers budget stamp duty as a percentage of the price they agreed. If the reckoner value for the flat is higher than that price, the duty is worked out on the reckoner value instead.

**The check is free and takes minutes.** Look the rate up on e-ASR, then enter it in our [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator-mumbai). It applies the higher figure for you.

The same value is the base for [stamp duty on a gift deed](/blog/gift-deed-stamp-duty-maharashtra).

## How to check the ready reckoner rate online

1. Open the **e-ASR** site of the Department of Registration and Stamps. It is linked from igrmaharashtra.gov.in.
2. Choose **Mumbai** on the map, then the year, for example 2026 to 2027.
3. Pick the district: **Mumbai Suburban** covers Borivali, Kandivali and Malad.
4. Choose the village. The names are in Marathi: बोरीवली for Borivali, कांदीवली for Kandivali and मालाड for Malad, which is split into East, South and North.
5. Find your building's zone in the list. Each zone is described by its roads and boundaries.
6. Read the **residential flat** column. The rate is in rupees per square metre.

The rates change each year on 1 April. So check that the year on the page matches the year you will register in.

## What if a flat sells below the reckoner rate?

Where a flat changes hands **well below** the reckoner value, the income tax rules treat the gap as if money had changed hands.

- For the **buyer**, section 56(2)(x) can tax the gap between the reckoner value and the price paid as the buyer's income.
- For the **seller**, section 50C can treat the reckoner value as the sale price when capital gains are worked out.

**There is a tolerance.** Since Budget 2020, if the gap is **not more than 10 percent**, neither section applies.

This is tax rather than property, so take the details to a chartered accountant. The useful point is simple: check the reckoner before you negotiate, and you will know the full tax picture of any price.

## Where the price and the reckoner sit apart

Across Mumbai, resale asking prices in 2025 to 2026 sat a median 103 percent above the reckoner rate for the same locality. That figure uses asking prices, not registered prices. So treat it as the direction of the gap rather than its exact size.

The gap is wider for new buildings than for old ones. One rate covers a whole zone, and a zone in Borivali or Kandivali holds both 30 year old walk ups and towers finished last year.

The reckoner does allow a deduction for a building's age:

- **Nothing** for a building under two years old
- **Rising to 70 percent** at sixty years
- Applied **only to the construction part** of the value, never the land

So within one zone, the reckoner sits closest to the real price on the oldest buildings. That is where an old flat sold at a low price can meet or pass the agreed figure.

We check the reckoner value for the specific building three times. First before a price is agreed, then when the agreement is drafted, and again if the deal crosses 1 April.

## Common questions

**Where do I check the reckoner rate?**
On the e-ASR site of the Department of Registration and Stamps, linked from the IGR Maharashtra portal. e-ASR stands for electronic Annual Statement of Rates. It is free and needs no account.

**What is the ready reckoner rate in Borivali West?**
For 2026 to 2027, the main residential zones in Borivali West range from 1,03,630 to 1,73,040 rupees per square metre. That is about 9,628 to 16,076 rupees per square foot. The rate depends on which zone the building is in.

**What if I buy below the reckoner rate?**
Stamp duty is still charged on the reckoner value. If the gap is more than 10 percent, income tax rules also apply: section 56(2)(x) to the buyer and section 50C to the seller.

**How often does it change?**
Every year. A new statement takes effect on 1 April, so a rate you checked last year may not be the rate you pay this year.

**Is the reckoner rate the same as the market rate?**
No. It is a government value for stamp duty. In most of these suburbs it sits well below what flats actually sell for, which is why it is checked building by building.
