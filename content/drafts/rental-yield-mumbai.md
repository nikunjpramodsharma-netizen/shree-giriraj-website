## Gross and net yield on a 2 crore flat

Rental yield in Mumbai means one thing: the rent you collect in a year, as a percentage of the money you put in.

Gross yield is the easy sum. Take the monthly rent, multiply by twelve, divide by the price, and multiply by a hundred. A flat bought for 2 crore rupees and let at 50,000 rupees a month earns 6 lakh rupees a year. That is a gross yield of 3 percent.

Gross yield rests on three assumptions, and all three are generous:

- The flat is never empty
- Owning it costs nothing
- The price was the only money you put in

Take the third one. On a 2 crore flat bought by a man, stamp duty is 12 lakh rupees and registration is 30,000 rupees. Your capital is already 2.12 crore before brokerage and fit out. The same 6 lakh of rent now gives 2.8 percent. The flat has not changed. Only the honest denominator has.

**Net yield** removes the generous assumptions. Take the rent you will actually collect, allowing for weeks between tenants. Subtract a year of running costs. Divide by everything you put in, not just the price.

- **Borivali** is an owner occupier suburb. Rents are steady, tenants are families, and they stay.

## Name the tenant before you name the building

The most useful question for a buyer who plans to let is: who will live in the flat? Three real examples from this belt:

- A family with a child at Thakur Public School wants a 2 BHK in Thakur Village, with a lift
- A couple working at Mindspace wants a 1 or 2 BHK near the Link Road, in a building that accepts late shifts
- A young professional at Andheri wants a 1 BHK within walking distance of a Metro 2A station in Kandivali West

Each is a repeatable tenant. Each rules some buildings in and others out. Once the tenant has a face, the flat and the rent follow.

## What comes off the rent

**Society maintenance.** This is charged monthly. In an older building with lifts and a watchman it is a real cost. It is the cost most often left out, because it feels like a household bill. The tenant rarely pays it.

**Property tax.** The BMC charges on capital value. A residential flat of up to 500 square feet carpet has paid nothing since January 2022. Above that size it is an annual cost.

**Repairs and replacement.** Paint between tenants, a geyser, a leaking tap. Budget an annual figure.

**Vacancy.** A flat between tenants earns nothing but still costs maintenance and tax. Four weeks a year takes about a month of rent out of the year. In a slow building it is more.

**Income tax.** Rent is taxable as income from house property. Section 24(a) of the Income Tax Act allows a standard deduction of thirty percent of the net annual value. Loan interest is deductible separately under section 24(b). The same rules now sit in section 22 of the Income Tax Act 2025, in force from 1 April 2026. The calculator on this site works before tax, because your rate depends on your own income. Your chartered accountant can tell you the rest.

## What goes into the capital

This half is often forgotten, and it moves the answer more than running costs do.

- **Stamp duty:** 6 percent for a male buyer and 5 percent for a woman in sole name, both including the metro cess. Joint ownership by a man and a woman pays 6 percent.
- **Registration:** 1 percent, capped at 30,000 rupees.
- **Brokerage:** paid once, agreed in writing.
- **Fit out:** whatever it costs to make the flat lettable.

Add these to the price and you have the figure the rent should be measured against.

## From gross to net, in five steps

1. Take twelve months of realistic rent.
2. Take off four to six weeks for the gap between tenants.
3. Take off maintenance, property tax and a repair allowance.
4. Add up the price, stamp duty, registration, brokerage and fit out.
5. Divide step 3 by step 4.

The [rental yield calculator](/tools/rental-yield-calculator) does this for you. It is always lower than gross, and knowing it before you buy turns a surprise into a plan.

## The society decides how fast it lets

In this belt, the first tenancy is more often delayed by the society than by the market. Some older societies have their own views on tenants, single people included. A few take weeks to issue a no objection certificate, which is the society's written permission. Before we show a landlord a flat to buy, we ask how the society treats tenants. Two flats can look identical on a listing, and one lets in a week while the other sits for two months.

The floor and the lane matter too. Tenants pay a little more for light, water pressure and a short walk to the station.

## What the law asks of a landlord

- **A registered agreement.** Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act requires every leave and licence agreement to be written and registered. The duty falls on the landlord. Registration is a morning's work, and we do it with every tenancy.
- **Police intimation.** Mumbai's societies and police expect the tenant's details to be shared, plus the society's no objection where it asks for one.
- **A deposit at the going rate.** Two to three months' rent is normal in newer societies. No Maharashtra law caps it. The [deposit article](/blog/rent-deposit-months-western-suburbs) explains why the two month figure you hear does not apply here. Write the return period of fifteen to thirty days into the agreement.
- **An inventory at handover.** It keeps the deposit conversation short at the end.

## Yield is only half of the return

A property returns two things: rent while you hold it, and what the market gives when you sell. Yield measures only the first.

A flat in an older building may let well and sell slowly. A newer one may show a lower yield and be far easier to sell. A high yield with no resale demand is hard to turn into cash. So use yield to check that the income is sound, then judge the building and the pocket separately.

## Common questions

**What is a good rental yield in Mumbai?**
Portals reported gross yields of about 2.5 to 4 percent for Borivali, Kandivali and Malad in 2026. Anarock put the city average at 4.3 percent. Whether a given flat is good depends on the building, the floor, the price you paid and what else you could do with the money. Work out the net figure for the specific flat.

**How much rent can I expect in Borivali, Kandivali or Malad?**
On 2026 portal figures, a 1 BHK asks roughly 18,000 to 50,000 rupees a month. A 2 BHK asks 35,000 to 82,000, and a 3 BHK 55,000 to a lakh. Malad East sits at the top of the 1 and 2 BHK ranges. Borivali and Kandivali West sit in the middle. We quote the achieved rent for your building, not the range.

**What is the difference between gross and net rental yield?**
Gross divides a year of rent by the purchase price with nothing taken off. Net takes maintenance, property tax, repairs and expected vacancy off the rent, and adds stamp duty, registration, brokerage and fit out to the capital. Net is what the flat actually pays you.

**Is rental yield calculated on carpet or built up area?**
Neither. Yield is calculated on money, not area. Rent and price are often quoted per square foot on different area bases, so work in total rupees. Area helps you compare two flats with each other, not to work out yield.

**Does rental income get taxed?**
Yes, as income from house property. Section 24(a) gives a standard deduction of thirty percent of the net annual value, and home loan interest is deductible under section 24(b). Your rate depends on your total income, so the calculator here works before tax.

**Is TDS deducted on rent?**
On a residential tenancy between individuals, usually not. Section 194-I applies where the payer is liable to deduct, and a company taking your flat for an employee will normally deduct. Ask at the agreement stage rather than in the first month's payment.

**Should the security deposit be counted in the yield?**
No. The deposit is money you hold and give back, not income. You can invest it while you hold it, but it does not belong in the rent line.

**Do I have to register the rent agreement?**
Yes. Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act requires it, and the duty is the landlord's.
