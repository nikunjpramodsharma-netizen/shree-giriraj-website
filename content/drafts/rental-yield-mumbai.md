## How do you calculate rental yield?

**Gross rental yield is the monthly rent times twelve, divided by the price, times a hundred.** Net yield is the same sum after costs come off the rent and go onto the price.

Gross yield rests on three generous assumptions:

- The flat is never empty
- Owning it costs nothing
- The price was the only money you put in

**Net yield** removes them. Take the rent you will actually collect. Subtract a year of running costs. Divide by everything you put in, not just the price.

---

## A worked example: a 650 sq ft 2 BHK in Borivali West

**On Square Yards' September 2026 figures, a 650 sq ft 2 BHK in Borivali West grosses about 2.98 percent.** It nets about 2.35 percent before property tax and repairs. Here is the sum, step by step.

| Step | Figure | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Price | 1,99,87,500 | 650 sq ft at Square Yards' average asking rate of 30,750 |
| Rent for a year | 5,95,200 | Square Yards' average 2 BHK rent of 49,600 a month, times 12 |
| **Gross yield on price** | **2.98 percent** | 5,95,200 divided by 1,99,87,500 |
| Stamp duty and registration | 12,29,250 | 6 percent duty for a man or a joint purchase, plus 30,000 registration |
| Money in | 2,12,16,750 | Price plus duty and registration |
| Rent collected, 11 months | 5,45,600 | One month allowed for a change of tenant |
| Society charges | 48,000 | Assumed at 4,000 a month for this example |
| Rent after those | 4,97,600 | |
| **Net yield** | **2.35 percent** | 4,97,600 divided by 2,12,16,750 |

The flat has not changed between the two lines. Only the honest denominator has. Property tax, repairs, brokerage and fit out would bring the net figure down a little more. The [rental yield calculator](/tools/rental-yield-calculator) adds them for any flat.

## Name the tenant before you name the building

**The most useful question for a buyer who plans to let is: who will live in the flat?** Three repeatable tenants from this belt:

- A family with a child at Thakur Public School wants a 2 BHK in Thakur Village, with a lift
- A couple working at Mindspace wants a 1 or 2 BHK near the Link Road, in a building that accepts late shifts
- A young professional at Andheri wants a 1 BHK within walking distance of a Metro 2A station in Kandivali West

Each one rules some buildings in and others out. Once the tenant has a face, the flat and the rent follow.

## What comes off the rent?

**Society maintenance, property tax, repairs and empty months all come off the rent before it reaches you.**

- **Society maintenance.** Charged monthly, and usually paid by the owner. In an older building with lifts and a watchman it is a real cost.
- **Property tax.** The BMC charges on capital value. A residential flat of up to 500 square feet carpet has paid none since January 2022. Above that size it is an annual cost. Our [BMC property tax calculator](/tools/bmc-property-tax-calculator) gives the figure.
- **Repairs.** Paint between tenants, a geyser, a leaking tap. Budget an annual figure.
- **Vacancy.** A flat between tenants earns nothing but still costs maintenance. Four weeks a year takes about a month of rent out of the year.

Rent is also taxable income. The calculator works before tax, so speak to your chartered accountant about your own position.

## What goes into the capital?

**Stamp duty, registration, brokerage and fit out all belong in the denominator.** This half is often forgotten, and it moves the answer more than running costs do.

- **Stamp duty:** 6 percent for a man or a joint purchase, and 5 percent for a woman in sole name, both including the 1 percent metro cess.
- **Registration:** 1 percent, capped at 30,000 rupees.
- **Brokerage:** paid once, agreed in writing.
- **Fit out:** whatever it costs to make the flat lettable.

The [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator-mumbai) gives the duty on any price.

## From gross to net, in five steps

1. Take twelve months of realistic rent.
2. Take off four to six weeks for the gap between tenants.
3. Take off maintenance, property tax and a repair allowance.
4. Add up the price, stamp duty, registration, brokerage and fit out.
5. Divide step 3 by step 4, and multiply by a hundred.

Net is always lower than gross, and knowing it before you buy turns a surprise into a plan.

## The society decides how fast a flat lets

**In this belt, the society's approach to tenants shapes the first tenancy as much as the market does.** Some older societies have their own views on tenants, single people included. ## What the law asks of a landlord

- **A registered agreement.** Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act requires every leave and licence agreement to be written and registered. The landlord registers it, and we do it with every tenancy.
- **Police intimation.** The tenant's details go to Mumbai Police on its online form. Our [police verification guide](/blog/tenant-police-verification-mumbai) has the steps.
- **A deposit at the going rate.** Two to three months' rent is normal in newer societies, and some older buildings ask more. No Maharashtra law caps it. The [deposit article](/blog/rent-deposit-months-western-suburbs) explains the norms.
- **An inventory at handover.** It keeps the deposit conversation short at the end.

## Yield is only half of the return

**A property returns rent while you hold it and whatever the market gives when you sell; yield measures only the first.** A flat in an older building may let well and sell slowly. A newer one may show a lower yield and sell far more easily. So use yield to check that the income is sound, then judge the building and the pocket on their own.

## Common questions

**What is a good rental yield in Mumbai?**
There is no official benchmark. Square Yards reported gross yields of 2.97 percent for Borivali West in September 2026 and 3.81 percent for Kandivali West in October 2026. Anarock put the Mumbai region average at 4.3 percent in mid 2026. Judge a flat on its net yield and its resale, not on gross alone.

**How do you calculate rental yield?**
Gross yield is a year's rent divided by the price, times a hundred. On a 2 crore rupee flat let at 49,600 a month, that is about 2.98 percent. Net yield takes costs off the rent and adds stamp duty and registration to the price, which gave 2.35 percent in our Borivali example.

**How much rent can I expect in Borivali, Kandivali or Malad?**
On 2026 NoBroker bands, a 1 BHK asks about 18,000 to 50,000 rupees a month and a 2 BHK 35,000 to 82,000. A 3 BHK asks 55,000 to a lakh. Malad East sits at the top of the ranges. We quote the achieved rent for your building, not the range.

**Where in Mumbai can I find the highest rental yield?**
Of the suburbs we cover, Kandivali West showed the higher portal figure in autumn 2026. Square Yards reported 3.81 percent there against 2.97 percent for Borivali West. The pocket, floor and price you pay move a flat's own yield more than the suburb average does.

**What is the ideal price to rent ratio?**
The price to rent ratio is the price divided by a year's rent, and it is the inverse of gross yield. At Borivali West's reported 2.97 percent, a flat costs about 34 years of rent. At Kandivali West's 3.81 percent, it costs about 26 years. A lower ratio means a higher yield.

**What is the difference between gross and net rental yield?**
Gross divides a year of rent by the purchase price with nothing taken off. Net takes off maintenance, property tax, repairs and expected vacancy. It also adds stamp duty, registration, brokerage and fit out to the capital. Net is what the flat actually pays you.

**Is rental yield calculated on carpet or built up area?**
Rental yield is calculated on money, not area. Rent and price are often quoted per square foot on different area bases, so work in total rupees. Area helps you compare two flats, not work out yield.

**Should the security deposit be counted in the yield?**
The security deposit is not income. It is money you hold and give back at the end of the tenancy, so it stays out of the rent line.
