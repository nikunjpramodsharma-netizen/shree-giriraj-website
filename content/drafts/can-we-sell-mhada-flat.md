## The short answer

Yes, a MHADA flat can be sold, but only with the previous written permission of the Board and of the housing society, and only once five years have passed from allotment, the buyer falls in the same income group as the scheme, and all dues are paid. **A sale without that permission can be declared invalid and the buyer evicted.**

---

## Where the rules come from

MHADA flats are not governed by the ordinary law of sale alone. They sit under the Estate Management, Sale, Transfer and Exchange of Tenements Regulations, 1981, made under the Maharashtra Housing and Area Development Act, 1976. The Regulations say two things that matter to a seller.

First, no allottee may transfer a tenement during the currency of the tenancy except with the previous permission in writing of the Board, and also of the housing society or company where one has been formed and registered. Both, not either.

Second, the Regulations set out when the society may give that permission: a period of five years has elapsed from the date of allotment; the transferee falls in the same income group as the scheme, so an Economically Weaker Section, Low Income Group or Middle Income Group flat goes to somebody in one of those groups and a High Income Group flat to somebody in that group; and the transferee has paid all dues to the Board.

That is the whole of the "five year rule" people search for. It is not a lock in that lifts automatically. It is the earliest point at which the society may consent, and the Board's permission is still needed on top.

---

## What the Board can ask for

The Regulations allow the Board to refuse permission, or to grant it subject to conditions. One condition is named specifically: payment of a portion of the unearned increase in the value of the tenement, not exceeding fifty percent of that increase, at the Authority's discretion.

Whether the Board asks for it, and how much, depends on the scheme and the flat. It is not a fixed levy and it is not always applied, but a seller who has not asked about it before agreeing a price can find the arithmetic of the deal changed after the fact. Ask first.

On top of that sits the transfer charge. The Mumbai Board's document list publishes a schedule for transfer of tenancy rights: 45 rupees per square foot for Low Income Group flats capped at 15,000 rupees, 55 rupees per square foot for Middle Income Group capped at 25,000 rupees, and 60 rupees per square foot for High Income Group capped at 35,000 rupees, payable by demand draft or pay order. Those figures are as published on mhada.gov.in and were checked on 9 September 2026. The Board revises them, so confirm the current schedule before paying.

---

## What happens if a MHADA flat is sold without permission?

The Regulations are explicit, and it is the most important paragraph on this page.

If an allottee transfers a tenement without the permissions above, the Board may declare the transfer invalid and of no effect, and the transfer stands cancelled. The transferee is deemed to be in unauthorised occupation and may be evicted under Chapter VI of the Act, after being given a chance to explain in writing. The Authority then refunds the amount the allottee paid, without interest, after deducting rent for the period the flat was occupied.

Read that from the buyer's side. They paid a market price for a flat, and the remedy on cancellation is a refund of what the original allottee paid the Board decades ago, less rent, to the allottee. The buyer's money is a matter between the buyer and the seller. That is why nobody should buy a MHADA flat on an unregistered agreement and a handshake, and why we will not act on one.

## The file the Board wants

The Mumbai Board publishes a fourteen item list for transfer of tenancy rights. The application is in a prescribed format from the Estate Manager's office. It is accompanied by a certificate stating the reason for the transfer, the no objection certificate of the original tenement holder, the society's no objection certificate or an affidavit that no society exists, an affidavit that the applicant and immediate family hold no other land or tenement in Mumbai city and suburbs, proof of income, attested photographs, the latest rent receipt showing no arrears, and the receipted transfer charges. The list says the application is to be submitted to the Estate Manager within fifteen days.

The items that trip people up are the income affidavit, because the buyer's income group has to fit the scheme, and the society NOC, because a society with a grievance can sit on it. Assemble the file before the agreement is signed, not after.

---

## Does this apply to shops in MHADA buildings?

No. The Regulations state that the part dealing with transfer of tenements does not apply to commercial tenements. A shop in a MHADA layout is a different matter with its own paperwork, and it is worth checking what the allotment actually says before assuming either way.

## Common questions

**Can I sell a MHADA flat after 5 years?**
Five years from allotment is the earliest point at which the society may permit a transfer. The Board's written permission is still needed, the buyer must fall in the scheme's income group, and dues must be clear. Five years is a condition, not a release.

**What are the MHADA transfer fees?**
The Mumbai Board's published schedule is 45 rupees per square foot for LIG capped at 15,000 rupees, 55 for MIG capped at 25,000, and 60 for HIG capped at 35,000, as checked on 9 September 2026. The Board may also make its permission conditional on a share of the unearned increase, up to fifty percent. Confirm both before you price the flat.

**What happens if a MHADA flat is sold without permission?**
The Board may declare the transfer invalid, treat the buyer as an unauthorised occupant and evict them, and refund the allottee's original payment without interest less rent. The buyer's purchase price is not refunded by the Board.

**Can a MHADA flat be gifted within the family?**
The Regulations provide for a tenement to be transferred in the name of another member of the allottee's family with the Chief Officer's permission, where the allottee is transferred in service, secures other accommodation, leaves the area, or wishes to transfer to a family member. It is still an application, with the family member undertaking to abide by the Regulations.

**Does the buyer need to be from the same income group?**
Yes. For EWS, LIG and MIG schemes the transferee must fall in one of those groups; for HIG schemes, in the same group. The income affidavit and proof in the Board's document list exist to establish that.

## Before you list the flat

Get the allotment letter, the latest rent receipt and the society's position in front of somebody who knows the Regulations. Find out whether the unearned increase applies to your scheme. Then price the flat, knowing what the transfer will cost and how long the Board has to decide.
