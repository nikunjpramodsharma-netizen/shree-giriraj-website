## The short answer

Yes. A MHADA flat can be sold with the previous written permission of both the Board and the housing society. Three conditions apply. Five years have passed since allotment. The buyer is in the same income group as the scheme. All dues to the Board are paid. **A sale without that permission can be cancelled by the Board.**

---

## What are the conditions and permissions for selling a MHADA flat?

Five things decide whether a sale goes through. Line them up before you list the flat.

| Condition or permission | What it means | Where to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board's written permission | The Board must agree before the sale, in writing | Estate Manager, Sale permission (Form 5) |
| Society's written permission | Needed as well, where a society is formed and registered | The society office |
| Five years from allotment | The earliest point at which the society may consent | Your allotment letter |
| Same income group | The buyer must fit the scheme's group (EWS, LIG, MIG or HIG) | The buyer's income affidavit and proof |
| Dues paid | The buyer clears all dues to the Board | The latest Board rent receipt |

Our guide to [MHADA dues explained](/blog/mhada-dues-lease-rent-property-tax) shows what the Board, the society and the BMC bill for.

You need both permissions, not either one. The five years are the minimum in the Regulations. Some schemes set a longer period in their own allotment terms, so the allotment letter is the first document we read.

## Where do the rules come from?

MHADA flats follow more than the ordinary law of sale. They sit under the Estate Management, Sale, Transfer and Exchange of Tenements Regulations, 1981. These were made under the Maharashtra Housing and Area Development Act, 1976. A tenement is the flat or unit the Board has let to the allottee.

The Regulations say two things that matter to a seller.

**First, you need permission.** No allottee may transfer a tenement during the tenancy without two written permissions. One is from the Board. The other is from the housing society or company, where one has been formed and registered.

**Second, the society may consent only when three things are true:**

- Five years have passed from the date of allotment.
- The buyer is in the same income group as the scheme. An Economically Weaker Section, Low Income Group or Middle Income Group flat goes to a buyer in one of those groups. A High Income Group flat goes to a buyer in that group.
- The buyer has paid all dues to the Board.

That is the whole of the "five year rule" people search for. It does not lift on its own. It is the earliest point at which the society may consent, and the Board's permission is still needed on top.

## What can the Board ask for?

The Regulations let the Board refuse permission or grant it with conditions. One condition is named: a share of the unearned increase, which is the rise in the flat's value since allotment. The share cannot be more than fifty percent, and it is at the Authority's discretion.

Whether the Board asks, and how much, depends on the scheme and the flat. It is not a fixed levy and it is not always applied. So ask before you agree a price, and the numbers stay as you planned them.

The Mumbai Board's document list also publishes a transfer charge for tenancy rights:

| Income group | Rate | Maximum charge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low Income Group (LIG) | 45 rupees per sq ft | 15,000 rupees |
| Middle Income Group (MIG) | 55 rupees per sq ft | 25,000 rupees |
| High Income Group (HIG) | 60 rupees per sq ft | 35,000 rupees |

It is paid by demand draft or pay order. These figures are as published on mhada.gov.in and were checked on 9 September 2026. The Board revises them, so confirm the current schedule before paying. ## How do you keep your sale valid?

Get both permissions before the sale is final. That is the single most useful step on this page.

The Regulations let the Board declare an unpermitted transfer invalid. In that case:

- The transfer stands cancelled.
- The buyer is treated as an unauthorised occupant. Under Chapter VI of the Act, the buyer may be asked to leave, after a chance to explain in writing.
- The Authority refunds the original allottee's payment, without interest, after deducting rent for the period of occupation.

Look at that from the buyer's side. The refund goes to the original allottee, not the buyer. The buyer's money is a matter between buyer and seller. This is why we do not act on an unregistered agreement and a handshake. With both permissions in hand first, the sale is clean for everyone.

## What file does the Board want?

The Mumbai Board publishes a fourteen item list for transfer of tenancy rights. The application is in a prescribed format, from the Estate Manager's office. It comes with:

- A certificate stating the reason for the transfer
- The no objection certificate of the original tenement holder
- The society's NOC, or an affidavit that no society exists
- An affidavit that the applicant and immediate family hold no other land or tenement in Mumbai city and suburbs
- Proof of income
- Attested photographs
- The latest rent receipt showing no arrears
- The receipted transfer charges

The list says the application goes to the Estate Manager within fifteen days.

Line up two items first. One is the income affidavit, because the buyer's income group has to fit the scheme. The other is the society NOC, because it can take time. Assemble the file before the agreement is signed.

## Does this apply to shops in MHADA buildings?

No. The Regulations state that the part dealing with transfer of tenements does not apply to commercial tenements. A shop in a MHADA layout has its own paperwork. Check what the allotment says before assuming either way.

## Common questions

**Can I sell a MHADA flat after 5 years?**
Five years from allotment is the earliest point at which the society may permit a transfer. The Board's written permission is still needed. The buyer must fall in the scheme's income group, and dues must be clear. Five years is a condition, not a release.

**What are the MHADA transfer fees?**
The Mumbai Board's published schedule is 45 rupees per square foot for LIG, capped at 15,000 rupees. It is 55 for MIG, capped at 25,000, and 60 for HIG, capped at 35,000. These were checked on 9 September 2026. The Board may also make its permission conditional on a share of the unearned increase, up to fifty percent. Confirm both before you price the flat.

**What happens if a MHADA flat is sold without permission?**
The Board may declare the transfer invalid and treat the buyer as an unauthorised occupant. It refunds the allottee's original payment without interest, less rent. The Board does not refund the buyer's purchase price.

**Can a MHADA flat be gifted within the family?**
Yes, with the Chief Officer's permission. The Regulations allow a tenement to move to another member of the allottee's family in these cases:

- The allottee is transferred in service.
- The allottee secures other accommodation.
- The allottee leaves the area.
- The allottee wishes to transfer to a family member.

It is still an application, and the family member undertakes to abide by the Regulations.

For the duty on a gift deed, see [stamp duty on a gift deed](/blog/gift-deed-stamp-duty-maharashtra).

**Does the buyer need to be from the same income group?**
Yes. For EWS, LIG and MIG schemes, the buyer must be in one of those groups. For HIG schemes, the buyer must be in the same group. The income affidavit and proof in the Board's document list establish this.
