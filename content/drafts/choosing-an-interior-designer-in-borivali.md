## Why a written scope matters

Most quotes arrive as one number for the whole flat. You cannot compare one number with another, because you cannot see what either contains.

A quote you can judge has three things in it:

- **Quantities:** twelve running feet of wardrobe, not "bedroom wardrobes"
- **Specifications:** the named board, finish and fittings
- **Exclusions:** what is not in the price

With those three, two quotes become comparable. The cheaper one stops looking better by default.

---

## Five questions to ask

### 1. What is the scope, in quantities?

Ask for running feet, square feet and unit counts. A designer who will not put quantities in writing before an advance has answered your question.

### 2. What is excluded?

This is where a budget is won or lost. These items are often outside a quote and arrive later as separate bills:

- Civil work, if walls are moving, and anything that needs the society's or the BMC's permission
- Electrical load upgrades and plumbing changes beyond the existing points
- Flooring, false ceiling and painting, which large firms price as separate lines
- In the kitchen, the sink, hob, chimney and counter top
- Loose furniture, curtains, light fittings and appliances
- Society charges: the refundable renovation deposit, lift use and debris removal
- GST at 18 percent, which published package prices leave out

### 3. Who is on site, and how often?

Design and execution are different jobs, often done by different people. Ask:

- Who supervises day to day?
- How often does the designer visit?
- Whom do you call when a carpenter has a question at 11 am on a Tuesday?

A weekly designer visit plus a supervisor on site every day is a stronger arrangement than a weekly visit with nobody else.

### 4. What happens if the work runs late?

Interiors often run late. The useful question is what happens when they do. A written completion date with an agreed consequence is worth more than an optimistic verbal one.

### 5. What has this person finished nearby?

Ask to see a finished flat in a nearby building, not a rendering. A rendering is a picture of a plan. A finished flat shows the work.

## How advances work

Advances are normal. The two largest organised firms publish what they take, which gives you a benchmark.

| Firm | Published payment terms |
| --- | --- |
| Livspace | 10 percent of the quote, or 60,000 rupees if that is more, to book. The balance falls due once 40 to 50 percent of the work is done on site |
| HomeLane | 10 percent to book. Another 10 percent before the site is masked. Then 30 percent of the woodwork and 80 percent of the non woodwork when the works contract is signed. The last 50 percent of the woodwork is due before the factory dispatches |

Read the pattern rather than the percentages. Both take a small booking amount first. Both freeze the design and quantities before the large payments. Both tie those payments to a signed contract or a dispatch, not to a date.

Before a second payment, these should exist:

1. Signed drawings
2. A bill of quantities, which is the itemised list of work and materials with named materials
3. A works contract with a completion date and an agreed consequence
4. The society's written permission

Money moves when those exist and something arrives or is installed, not when a calendar page turns. If a designer asks for 30 or 40 percent before the drawings are signed, ask what that money buys on the day you pay it.

## What a Mumbai society will let you do

The rule is in the model bye laws every registered society adopts. Bye law 46 says no member may add to or alter a flat without the committee's prior written permission. A structural change needs the permission of the competent authority as well, which in Mumbai means the BMC.

That splits interior work into two lists.

**Needs the society's written no objection only:**

- Tiling
- A bathroom renovated in the same place
- False ceilings and painting
- Rewiring
- A modular kitchen
- Non load bearing partitions, meaning walls that do not hold up the building

**Needs the BMC as well, under section 342 of the Municipal Corporation Act:**

- Touching a column, beam or load bearing wall
- Combining two flats
- Moving a bathroom or kitchen, because the drainage moves too
- Enclosing a balcony
- Adding a mezzanine

Societies often refuse the second list, along with any work that changes the building's outside face or a neighbour's plumbing.

The society's letter usually comes with conditions. They are much the same across the belt:

- An application ten to fifteen days ahead, with the scope and the contractor's identity proofs
- A refundable deposit, which most societies set between 7,500 and 50,000 rupees
- An indemnity bond, a written promise to cover the society's loss if the work causes damage
- Work between about nine and one, and again from two or three until six
- Heavy drilling and breaking kept to the late morning
- Nothing on Sundays or public holidays
- Debris bagged and out within a day
- Lift use either restricted or charged for

Get the letter before the first bag of cement arrives. A committee can stop work that starts without it, and a stopped site costs more per day than the permission would have.

## Common questions

**Do I need society permission for interior work?**
Yes, in writing, from the committee, before work starts, under bye law 46 of the model bye laws. For anything structural you need the BMC as well, and the society will not sign off without it.

**How much advance is normal for interior work?**
About 10 percent to book, which is what the largest organised firms publish. The large payments follow once the design and quantities are signed. If a designer asks for more before anything is in writing, ask what it covers.

**What is not included in an interior quote?**
See the exclusions list above. In short: structural work, electrical load beyond the existing points, and loose items are usually separate.

**How much does a 2 BHK interior cost in Mumbai?**
It depends on the specification more than on the flat, and the gap between basic and premium is wide. We give an itemised estimate for your flat before work starts, plus 18 percent GST. List your quantities with the [interior scope builder](/tools/interior-scope-builder) and the estimate comes quicker and closer.

**How much do interior designers charge in Mumbai?**
In two ways. Organised firms fold the design fee into the package price. Independent designers charge per square foot or a percentage of the project value. Ask which applies before the first drawing, and get it in writing.
