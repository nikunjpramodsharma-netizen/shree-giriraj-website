## How do you choose an interior designer?

**Choose the designer whose quote you can read line by line and who can show you a finished flat nearby.** A quote you can judge has three things in it:

- **Quantities:** twelve running feet of wardrobe, not "bedroom wardrobes"
- **Specifications:** the named board, finish and fittings
- **Exclusions:** what is not in the price

With those three, two quotes become comparable, and you can see exactly what each one buys.

---

## Seven questions to ask before you pay an advance

### 1. What is the scope, in quantities?

**Ask for running feet, square feet and unit counts in writing.** A designer who prices in quantities gives you a quote you can compare and a list to check at handover.

### 2. What is excluded?

**The exclusions list is where you protect your budget.** These items often sit outside a quote:

- Civil work, if walls are moving, and anything that needs the society's or the BMC's permission
- Electrical load upgrades and plumbing changes beyond the existing points
- Flooring, false ceiling and painting, which large firms price as separate lines
- In the kitchen, the sink, hob, chimney and countertop
- Loose furniture, curtains, light fittings and appliances
- Society charges: the refundable renovation deposit, lift use and debris removal
- GST at 18 percent, which published package prices often leave out

### 3. Who is on site, and how often?

**Ask who supervises day to day and how often the designer visits.** Design and execution are different jobs, often done by different people. A weekly designer visit plus a supervisor on site every day is the stronger arrangement. Ask also whom you call when a carpenter has a question at 11 am on a Tuesday.

### 4. When will it finish?

**Ask for a written completion date with an agreed consequence.** Design Cafe's September 2026 guide gives 30 to 45 days for factory made work after design sign off. Civil work adds 2 to 3 weeks. A date in the contract is worth more than an optimistic verbal one.

### 5. What has this designer finished nearby?

**Ask to see a finished flat in a nearby building, not a rendering.** A rendering is a picture of a plan. A finished flat shows the work.

### 6. How is the design fee charged?

**Ask whether the fee is inside the package or charged separately.** Livspace's January 2026 Mumbai guide says its cost includes the designer's fee. Other designers may charge a separate fee, so get the basis in writing before the first drawing.

### 7. Who handles the society?

**Ask who applies for the society's written permission.** Work cannot start without it, and the application needs the scope and the contractor's details. We handle it for our clients.

## How much advance is normal for interior work?

**About 10 percent to book, on the payment terms the two largest organised firms publish.** Both read on 16 November 2026:

| Firm | Published payment terms |
| --- | --- |
| Livspace | 10 percent of the final quote, or 49,000 rupees if that is higher, to book. The remaining 60 percent of the order falls due when 40 to 50 percent of the work is done on site |
| HomeLane | 10 percent to book. Another 10 percent before the site is masked. Then 30 percent of the woodwork and 80 percent of the non woodwork when the works contract is signed. The last 50 percent of the woodwork is due before the factory dispatches |

Read the pattern rather than the percentages. Both take a small booking amount first. Both freeze the design and quantities before the large payments. Both tie those payments to a signed contract or a dispatch, not to a date.

Before a second payment, these should exist:

1. Signed drawings
2. A bill of quantities, the itemised list of work and named materials
3. A works contract with a completion date and an agreed consequence
4. The society's written permission

Then money moves as work is delivered and installed. If a designer asks for 30 or 40 percent before the drawings are signed, ask what that money buys on the day you pay it.

## What a Mumbai society will let you do

**Bye law 46 of the model bye laws says no member may add to or alter a flat without the committee's prior written permission.** Every registered society adopts these bye laws. A structural change needs the competent authority as well, which in Mumbai means the BMC.

That splits interior work into two lists.

**Needs the society's written no objection only:**

- Tiling
- A bathroom renovated in the same place
- False ceilings and painting
- Rewiring
- A modular kitchen
- Non load bearing partitions, meaning walls that do not hold up the building

**Needs the BMC as well, under section 342 of the Municipal Corporation Act:**

- Touching a column, beam or load bearing wall
- Combining two flats
- Moving a bathroom or kitchen, because the drainage moves too
- Enclosing a balcony
- Adding a mezzanine

The society's letter usually comes with conditions. Published summaries of Mumbai society rules list much the same ones:

- An application ten to fifteen days ahead, with the scope and the contractor's identity proofs
- A refundable deposit, which most societies set between 7,500 and 50,000 rupees
- An indemnity bond, a written promise to cover the society's loss if the work causes damage
- Work between about nine and one, and again from two or three until six
- Heavy drilling kept to the late morning
- Nothing on Sundays or public holidays
- Debris bagged and out within a day
- Lift use either restricted or charged for

Get the letter before the first bag of cement arrives, and the work runs without a pause.

## Common questions

**How do I choose an interior designer?**
Choose on a written scope with quantities, a clear exclusions list, a named site supervisor and a completion date in the contract. See one finished flat in person. Then compare quotes line by line, not on the total.

**How much do interior designers charge in Mumbai?**
Large firms usually fold the design fee into the package, so you pay for the work. Design Cafe's September 2026 guide gives ₹1.5 to 4 lakh for a kitchen and ₹60,000 to ₹1.5 lakh per wardrobe. A whole 2 BHK runs about ₹5.5 to 12 lakh and above, plus GST.

**How much do interior designers charge per room?**
Design Cafe's room figures are ₹1.5 to 4 lakh for a kitchen and ₹60,000 to ₹1.5 lakh per wardrobe. Living and dining run ₹1 to 2 lakh. Both bedrooms together run ₹3 to 4 lakh and upwards.

**How much advance is normal for interior work?**
About 10 percent to book, which is what Livspace and HomeLane publish. Livspace's minimum booking is 49,000 rupees. The large payments follow signed drawings and a works contract.

**Do I need society permission for interior work?**
Yes, in writing, from the committee, before work starts, under bye law 46 of the model bye laws. For anything structural you need the BMC as well, and the society will not sign off without it.

**What is not included in an interior quote?**
Civil work, new electrical points, plumbing shifts, appliances, loose furniture and often GST are outside many quotes. See the exclusions list above and ask for yours in writing.

**How long does interior work take?**
Design Cafe gives 30 to 45 days for factory made work after design sign off, plus 2 to 3 weeks of civil work. Write the date into your contract.
