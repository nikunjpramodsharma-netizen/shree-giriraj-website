## The short answer

Carpet area is the usable floor space inside your flat, wall to wall. Built up area adds the thickness of the walls. Super built up area adds your share of the lobby, lift and staircase. **You pay on super built up, you live on carpet**, and the gap between them is called loading.

---

## Three numbers for the same flat

Every flat has three areas, and a brochure will usually lead with the largest one. They are not interchangeable and the difference is not small.

**Carpet area** is what it sounds like. If you could lay carpet across the whole flat, that is the area it would cover. Under RERA it is measured wall to wall inside the flat, and it excludes the external walls and any common area. It includes the internal partition walls between your own rooms. **Built up area** is carpet area plus the thickness of the walls, and usually a share of the balcony. It is typically around 10 to 15 percent more than carpet. [ VERIFY: that 10 to 15 percent range against a real example. Do not publish a range you cannot point at. ]

**Super built up area** is built up area plus your proportional share of everything the building has in common: the lobby, the lift shaft, the staircase, the club house, sometimes the generator room. This is the number most quoted prices are based on.

## What loading actually means for your money

Loading is the percentage by which super built up exceeds carpet. If a flat is quoted at 1,000 square feet super built up with 30 percent loading, you are living on about 700 square feet.

That matters in one specific way that people miss. **The rate per square foot is quoted on super built up, so two flats at the same rate can give you very different homes.** A flat at ₹25,000 per square foot with 25 percent loading is better value than one at ₹24,000 with 40 percent loading, even though the second looks cheaper.

The arithmetic is simple and worth doing before you get emotionally attached to a flat:

1. Take the quoted price and divide by the **carpet** area, not the quoted area
2. Do the same for the flat you are comparing it to
3. Compare those two numbers, not the headline rate

## What the law says now

Since RERA came into force, a builder has to declare carpet area for any registered project, and the sale agreement has to state it. That does not stop anyone quoting a price on super built up, and most still do. It does mean the carpet number exists in writing somewhere, and you are entitled to it before you commit.

## Common questions

**Is carpet area the same as usable area?**
Close, but not identical. Carpet area under RERA includes internal partition walls. Usable area is a looser term with no legal definition, so it is worth asking what somebody means when they use it.

**What is a good loading percentage?**
Nationally, 25 to 35 percent is widely described as normal and anything above 40 percent as high. **Mumbai runs the highest loading in India, commonly 40 to 50 percent**, so a figure that looks alarming by national standards can be ordinary here. Sources checked 1 September 2026.

## Before you sign

The area on the brochure is the least reliable number in the transaction. Ask for the carpet area in writing, do the price per carpet foot calculation yourself, and compare flats on that basis rather than on the headline rate.
