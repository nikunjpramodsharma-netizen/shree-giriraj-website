## The short answer

A housing society redevelops under State directions under Section 79A of the Cooperative Societies Act. Reports on the 30 September 2026 resolution say a developer needs written approval from at least 51 percent of all members. Members get a flat nearby, rent plus deposit, or transit housing, until the new flat is ready. **The corpus fund is agreed in writing.**

---

## What does Section 79A have to do with redevelopment?

Section 79A lets the state give directions that a cooperative society must follow. In a February 2026 ruling on transfer premiums, the Bombay High Court held that such directions bind societies. Redevelopment has been run through these directions for years.

The latest is a Government Resolution of the Cooperation Department dated 30 September 2026. A law firm's note says it replaces the resolution of 4 July 2019. The Maharashtra Cooperative Societies (Amendment) Rules, 2026, notified on 18 June 2026, put some safeguards into the Rules themselves.

We could not open the resolution on the state's own portal. So the points below come from a law firm's note and trade press. **Ask your society's secretary for a copy of the resolution and read it before the vote.**

---

## Who has to agree, and how fast must the building be ready?

Here is what the reports on the 2026 resolution say:

- A developer can be appointed only with written approval from at least 51 percent of all members.
- Only members present at the meeting count toward that vote. Members who cannot attend in person can join by video.
- The society invites at least three competitive bids.
- A developer should finish within two years of the first plinth or foundation certificate. In exceptional cases the period can stretch to three years.

A plinth certificate is the municipal permission to build above the foundation. The two year clock matters to you because every extra month is a month of rent for the developer to pay.

## What is a corpus fund and how much should it be?

A corpus fund is a lump sum the developer pays the society. It is usually set aside as a deposit. Its interest can help meet the society's costs after the move back.

The sources we read do not set a figure. The amount is negotiated and written into the development agreement. Treat any number quoted online as one deal, not a rule.

Here is how the sum works, using a stated assumption. This is arithmetic, not a market rate.

| Assumption | Working | Corpus held by the society |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 40 members, 3,00,000 rupees each | 40 × 3,00,000 | 1,20,00,000 rupees |
| 40 members, 5,00,000 rupees each | 40 × 5,00,000 | 2,00,00,000 rupees |
| 60 members, 5,00,000 rupees each | 60 × 5,00,000 | 3,00,00,000 rupees |

Ask three things before you agree a number:

1. Who holds the money, and in which account?
2. What may the society spend it on?
3. When is it paid: at signing or on handover?

## How much monthly rent will you get in Borivali and Kandivali?

The reports say the developer should, as far as possible, give members alternative accommodation in the same area. If not, it pays monthly rent plus a deposit acceptable to members, or provides transit housing. The resolution as reported gives no rupee figure. The rent is agreed with the society.

A fair way to set it is to look at what a similar flat rents for nearby. These are the asking bands on our area pages, from NoBroker's 2026 data:

## Where can you live while the building is rebuilt?

You have four routes:

- **A flat in the same area from the developer.** The reports put this first, as far as possible.
- **Transit housing from the developer.** The reports list this as another option. Ask for the address, the size and the date it is ready.
- **A rented flat nearby with the developer's rent.** This keeps children in the same school and the same Borivali or Kandivali routine.
- **A rented flat in a new project.** Our [rentals service](/services/rentals) finds owner verified flats.

When a whole building moves at once, many families look for flats in the same weeks. Start looking three to four months before the vacate date. That gives you time to compare societies on our [Borivali West](/areas/borivali-west) and [Kandivali](/areas/kandivali) pages.

## What should the agreement say about the new flat?

The agreement should name the new flat in plain terms. Check these points:

1. **The carpet area of the new flat.** Carpet area is the floor space inside your walls. Our guide to [carpet area and built up area](/blog/carpet-area-vs-built-up-area) shows how to read the number.
2. **The date of possession,** with rent continuing until the occupancy certificate arrives. The [occupancy certificate](/blog/occupancy-certificate) is the municipality's permission to live in the finished building.
3. **Rent for each month of delay.**
4. **The land position.** Our guide to [conveyance and deemed conveyance](/blog/conveyance-and-deemed-conveyance) explains why a society should hold clear title first.

A society in a MHADA layout follows a separate route through the Board. Read our guide to [MHADA redevelopment rules](/blog/mhada-redevelopment-rules) first.

## Common questions

**What is a corpus fund in redevelopment?**
It is a lump sum the developer pays the society, often kept as a deposit. The amount is agreed in the development agreement. The sources we read set no fixed figure.

**How much rent does a developer pay during redevelopment?**
The rent is agreed with the society. A fair guide is the going rent for a similar flat nearby. Asking rents in 2026 were 35,000 to 65,000 rupees for a 2 BHK in Borivali and Kandivali West.

**How many members must agree to redevelopment?**
Reports on the 30 September 2026 resolution say at least 51 percent of all members must approve the developer in writing. Read the resolution itself for the meeting rules.

**How long should redevelopment take?**
The reports say a developer should finish within two years of the first plinth or foundation certificate. In exceptional cases it can stretch to three years.

**Does the developer have to pay a deposit as well as rent?**
The reports say the developer first tries to give a flat in the same area. If not, it pays monthly rent plus a deposit acceptable to members, or provides transit housing. Agree the deposit amount in writing.

**Is a society in a MHADA layout treated differently?**
Yes. It also needs the Board's no objection and a clear lease position. Our guide to [MHADA redevelopment rules](/blog/mhada-redevelopment-rules) covers the order of steps.

**Does a member who misses the meeting count toward the 51 percent?**
No, as the reports describe it. Only members present count, and the vote of an absent member is not counted. Members who cannot attend in person can join by video.

**Where can a tenant of the old building find rental housing?**
