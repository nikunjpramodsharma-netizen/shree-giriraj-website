## The short answer

Housing society redevelopment in Maharashtra follows State guidelines under Section 79A, revised on 30 September 2026. As reported, a developer needs written approval from at least 51 percent of all members. Members get a flat nearby, rent plus a deposit, or transit housing until the new flat is ready. **The corpus fund is negotiated and written into the agreement.**

---

## What does Section 79A have to do with redevelopment?

**Section 79A lets the state issue directions to cooperative societies, and redevelopment has run on those directions since 2019.** The first set was a Government Resolution of 4 July 2019. IndiaLaw's note, updated 4 November 2026, says a new resolution of the Cooperation Department dated 30 September 2026 replaces it.

How strictly do the courts apply them? The Bombay High Court has called the redevelopment guidelines directory, a road map rather than a rigid rule. SupremeToday reported that ruling on 1 June 2026, in a Goregaon case (Devendra Kumar Jain, Ramanuj CHS). The court looked for substantial compliance and a clear, informed majority. So following the guidelines closely is still the safest route for a society.

The Maharashtra Cooperative Societies (Amendment) Rules, 2026, notified on 18 June 2026 as Trilegal reported, also put some safeguards into the Rules themselves.

The resolution itself sits behind a captcha on the state's GR portal, so the terms below are the reports' words, each named. **Ask your society's secretary for a copy of the resolution and read it before the vote.**

## Who has to agree, and how fast must the building be ready?

**At least 51 percent of all members must approve the developer in writing, and the building should be ready within two to three years.** Here is what each report says about the 2026 resolution:

- **Approval:** a developer needs written approval from at least 51 percent of total members, says IndiaLaw's note of 4 November 2026.
- **Who counts:** members present in person or by video count, and an absent member's vote does not, per the same note.
- **Quorum:** two thirds of members, with at least 51 percent physically present, the note says. Construction World's report of 8 October 2026 gives the same two thirds quorum.
- **Bids:** the society should seek at least three competitive bids, Construction World reports.
- **Deadline:** Construction World reports two years from the first plinth or foundation certificate, and three years at most in exceptional cases.

A plinth certificate is the municipal permission to build above the foundation. The two year clock matters to you because every extra month is a month of rent for the developer to pay.

## What else does the 2026 resolution give members?

**More documents, firmer deadlines and a clearer say, according to IndiaLaw's note of 4 November 2026.** The note lists these changes:

| What the note reports | Why it helps a member |
| --- | --- |
| Only a duly elected managing committee can run redevelopment | Decisions stay with the people members voted in |
| Redevelopment papers open to members for inspection free of charge | You can read the tenders and the draft agreement yourself |
| The bank guarantee is set by the general body, no longer fixed at 20 percent | Members negotiate the security the developer gives |
| Each member's alternative accommodation agreement within 3 months of the development agreement's registration | Your own flat terms are in writing early |
| Membership and share certificates for new flat holders within 3 months of possession | Buyers of the new flats join the society quickly |

Each of these is worth checking against the resolution's own text before the vote.

## What is a corpus fund and how much should it be?

**A corpus fund is a lump sum the developer pays the society, and its size is negotiated, not fixed by law.** It is usually set aside as a deposit. Its interest can help meet the society's costs after the move back.

The sources we read do not set a figure. The amount is negotiated and written into the development agreement. Treat any number quoted online as one deal, not a rule.

Here is how the sum works, using a stated assumption. This is arithmetic, not a market rate.

| Assumption | Working | Corpus held by the society |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 40 members, 3,00,000 rupees each | 40 × 3,00,000 | 1,20,00,000 rupees |
| 40 members, 5,00,000 rupees each | 40 × 5,00,000 | 2,00,00,000 rupees |
| 60 members, 5,00,000 rupees each | 60 × 5,00,000 | 3,00,00,000 rupees |

Ask three things before you agree a number:

1. Who holds the money, and in which account?
2. What may the society spend it on?
3. When is it paid: at signing or on handover?

## How much monthly rent will you get in Borivali and Kandivali?

**The rent is agreed with the society, and the fair benchmark is what a similar flat nearby rents for.** Construction World reported on 8 October 2026 that the developer should, as far as possible, give members a flat in the same area. If not, it pays monthly rent plus a deposit acceptable to members, or provides transit housing. The reports give no rupee figure.

## What should the agreement say about the new flat?

**The carpet area, the possession date, rent for any delay and the land position.** The agreement should name the new flat in plain terms. Check these points:

1. **The carpet area of the new flat.** Carpet area is the floor space inside your walls. Our guide to [carpet area and built up area](/blog/carpet-area-vs-built-up-area) shows how to read the number.
2. **The date of possession,** with rent continuing until the occupancy certificate arrives. The [occupancy certificate](/blog/occupancy-certificate) is the municipality's permission to live in the finished building.
3. **Rent for each month of delay.**
4. **The land position.** Our guide to [conveyance and deemed conveyance](/blog/conveyance-and-deemed-conveyance) explains why a society should hold clear title first.

A society in a MHADA layout follows a separate route through the Board. Read our guide to [MHADA redevelopment rules](/blog/mhada-redevelopment-rules) first.

## After how many years can a building be redeveloped in Mumbai?

**No minimum age was set in the redevelopment sources we read; the 30 year mark matters because of the structural audit.** Section 353B of the Mumbai Municipal Corporation Act asks every building to get a structural stability certificate. It is due within one year of the building turning 30, and every 10 years after.

That certificate, from a structural engineer registered with the BMC, is often what starts a society's redevelopment conversation. Ask your committee for the latest one before any vote.

## Common questions

**What is a corpus fund in redevelopment?**
It is a lump sum the developer pays the society, often kept as a deposit. The amount is agreed in the development agreement. The sources we read set no fixed figure.

**How much rent does a developer pay during redevelopment?**
The rent is agreed with the society. A fair guide is the going rent for a similar flat nearby. Asking rents in 2026 were 35,000 to 65,000 rupees for a 2 BHK in Borivali and Kandivali West.

**How many members must agree to redevelopment?**
At least 51 percent of all members, in writing, according to IndiaLaw's note of 4 November 2026 on the 30 September 2026 resolution. Read the resolution itself for the meeting rules.

**How long should redevelopment take?**
Construction World reported on 8 October 2026 that a developer should finish within two years of the first plinth or foundation certificate. In exceptional cases it can stretch to three years.

**Does the developer have to pay a deposit as well as rent?**
Construction World's report says the developer first tries to give a flat in the same area. If not, it pays monthly rent plus a deposit acceptable to members, or provides transit housing. Agree the deposit amount in writing.

**Is a society in a MHADA layout treated differently?**
Yes. It also needs the Board's no objection and a clear lease position. Our guide to [MHADA redevelopment rules](/blog/mhada-redevelopment-rules) covers the order of steps.

**Does a member who misses the meeting count toward the 51 percent?**
No, as IndiaLaw's note describes it. Members present in person or by video count, and an absent member's vote is not counted.

**Are the Section 79A redevelopment guidelines binding?**
The Bombay High Court has called them directory, a road map, in a ruling SupremeToday reported on 1 June 2026. It looked for substantial compliance and a clear majority, so following them closely remains the safe course.

**After how many years can a building be redeveloped in Mumbai?**
The sources we read set no minimum age. Section 353B of the Mumbai Municipal Corporation Act requires a structural stability certificate once a building turns 30, and every 10 years after.
