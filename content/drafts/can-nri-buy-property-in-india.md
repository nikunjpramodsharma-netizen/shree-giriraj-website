## The short answer

Yes. A non resident Indian (NRI) or Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) may buy a flat, shop or office in India. That is the rule in the RBI's FEMA regulations of 2018. Agricultural land, farmhouses and plantation property are excluded. **Payment must come through banking channels or an NRE, NRO or FCNR(B) account.**

FEMA is the Foreign Exchange Management Act, the law that governs money moving in and out of India. The RBI is the Reserve Bank of India, which writes the detailed rules under it.

---

## Who counts as an NRI or an OCI?

The RBI's regulations define both terms in regulation 2.

- **NRI:** a person resident outside India who is a citizen of India. You keep your Indian passport and live abroad.
- **OCI:** a person resident outside India who is registered as an Overseas Citizen of India card holder. The card is for people of Indian origin who hold another passport.

For buying a home, the regulations treat an NRI and an OCI the same way. So everything below applies to both. Our companion guide, [buying a flat in Mumbai from abroad](/blog/buy-flat-in-mumbai-from-abroad), covers the practical steps.

---

## What can an NRI or OCI buy?

Regulation 3 of the 2018 regulations sets out what an NRI or OCI may do. The table gives it in plain words.

| What you want to do | Allowed? | The rule in plain words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buy a flat, house, shop or office | Yes | Any immovable property except farmland, a farmhouse or plantation property |
| Receive such property as a gift | Yes | From a resident Indian, an NRI or an OCI who is your relative |
| Inherit property | Yes | From a resident Indian, or from a non resident who bought it lawfully |
| Sell to a resident Indian | Yes | Through banking channels |
| Buy agricultural land, a farmhouse or a plantation | No | Excluded from the purchase route |

The regulations set no cap on how many flats you may buy. The limit you will meet is on sending sale money abroad, which a later section explains.

Immovable property means land and buildings, things that cannot be moved.

---

## Is RBI approval needed to buy a flat?

No separate approval is needed for a residential or commercial purchase, because regulation 3 itself permits it. Approval is needed for cases outside that list.

One such case is set out in regulation 9. Citizens of Pakistan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Afghanistan, China, Iran, Nepal, Bhutan, Hong Kong, Macau and North Korea need the RBI's prior permission. Regulation 9 adds that this does not apply to OCI card holders.

---

## Can an NRI or OCI buy agricultural land, a farmhouse or a plantation?

No. The RBI's regulations and its FAQ both exclude agricultural land, farmhouses and plantation property from what an NRI or OCI can buy. The answer is the same for NRIs and OCIs.

One route stays open. You may inherit such land, and the RBI's FAQ says agricultural land may be sold only to a resident Indian.

A farm plot or farmhouse offered to an NRI as a "purchase" is a reason to pause and ask. Our own work is flats, shops and offices in Borivali, Kandivali and Malad.

## How does an NRI pay for a flat?

Regulation 3 says payment must come by inward remittance through banking channels, or from a non resident account. Inward remittance means money sent to India from your overseas bank account. The RBI's FAQ lists the accounts: NRE, FCNR(B) and NRO.

- **NRE account:** a non resident external account, held in rupees, funded from money you earn abroad.
- **FCNR(B) account:** a foreign currency deposit account that a non resident can hold with an Indian bank.
- **NRO account:** a non resident ordinary account, for income earned in India, such as rent.

Regulation 3 rules out payment by travellers' cheque or by foreign currency notes. Regulation 11 requires every such deal to go through banking channels in India, so cash is out too.

## Can a foreign citizen who is not of Indian origin buy?

Generally only with the RBI's permission. The RBI's FAQ lets a foreign national living outside India take a lease of up to five years, or inherit from a resident Indian. Any other purchase needs the RBI's prior permission.

A foreign national living in India may buy, unless they are a citizen of one of the 11 countries listed above. Regulation 6 also lets a foreign spouse of an NRI or OCI buy one property jointly with them. The marriage must be registered and must have lasted at least two years.

1. **A new project must be registered with MahaRERA.** MahaRERA is the state regulator for new projects. ## Selling later: how much can you send abroad?

Sale money lands in an Indian account first. What can go abroad depends on how you paid for the flat.

- **You paid with money sent from abroad, or from an NRE or FCNR(B) account.** Regulation 8 lets your bank send the sale money abroad. For homes, this covers up to two residential properties.
- **Other money, such as an NRO balance.** The RBI's Master Direction on Remittance of Assets lets a bank send up to USD 1 million a financial year, on documents. A financial year runs from April to March.
- **OCI card holders.** The same Master Direction counts an OCI card holder as a person of Indian origin. So the USD 1 million route covers OCIs as well as NRIs.

Tax applies to NRI purchases and sales, so speak to a chartered accountant before you list.

## Common questions

**Can an NRI buy a flat in Mumbai without visiting India?**
Yes, an NRI can buy a Mumbai flat without visiting India, by signing a power of attorney abroad and paying through the bank. The RBI rules do not require a visit. Our guide on [buying a flat in Mumbai from abroad](/blog/buy-flat-in-mumbai-from-abroad) gives the steps.

**Is RBI permission needed to buy a flat as an NRI?**
No separate RBI permission is needed for an NRI to buy a flat in India. Regulation 3 of the RBI's 2018 regulations already allows an NRI or OCI to buy any property except agricultural land, a farmhouse or plantation property.

**How many flats can an NRI buy in India?**
The RBI's 2018 regulations set no limit on the number of flats an NRI or OCI may buy. The limit is on sending sale money abroad: money paid in from abroad can go back out for up to two residential properties.

**Can an OCI card holder buy a flat in India?**
Yes, an OCI card holder can buy a flat in India on the same terms as an NRI, under regulation 3. Agricultural land, farmhouses and plantation property are excluded. The RBI's list of restricted countries does not apply to OCI card holders.

**Can an NRI buy agricultural land in India?**
No, an NRI or OCI cannot buy agricultural land, a farmhouse or plantation property in India under the RBI's 2018 regulations. Inheriting such land is allowed. The RBI's FAQ says farmland may be sold only to a resident Indian.

**Can an NRI buy a MHADA flat?**
The RBI rules allow an NRI to buy a residential flat, and they do not single out MHADA flats. The Board's own permissions for the sale still apply. We prepare that file for MHADA flats anywhere in Mumbai.

**Can an NRI receive a flat as a gift or inheritance?**
Yes, an NRI or OCI may receive a flat as a gift or an inheritance under regulation 3. A gift must come from a relative, who may be a resident Indian, an NRI or an OCI. An inheritance may come from a resident Indian, or from a non resident who bought lawfully.

**Which account should an NRI pay from?**
An NRI pays by inward remittance through the bank, or from an NRE, FCNR(B) or NRO account, as the RBI's FAQ sets out. Travellers' cheques and foreign currency notes are not allowed. Keep a written receipt for every payment.

**Can a foreign citizen who is not of Indian origin buy a flat in India?**
A foreign citizen of non Indian origin living outside India needs the RBI's prior permission to buy. Inheritance and a lease of up to five years are the exceptions. A foreign spouse of an NRI or OCI may buy one property jointly with them.

**How much money can an NRI send abroad after selling a flat?**
An NRI or OCI can send up to USD 1 million a financial year from an NRO balance. That limit is in the RBI's Master Direction on Remittance of Assets. Sale money from a flat bought with funds from abroad can go back out under regulation 8, for up to two homes.
