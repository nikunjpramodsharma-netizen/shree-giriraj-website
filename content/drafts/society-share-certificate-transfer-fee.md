## The short answer

A share certificate proves you are a member of your housing society and hold its shares. It is not the title to the flat, which comes from the registered agreement. To transfer it, the society needs 15 days' written notice under Rule 24. **The seller pays a 500 rupee transfer fee and a premium capped at 25,000 rupees in Mumbai.**

---

## What does a society share certificate prove?

A cooperative housing society is owned by its flat owners. Each member holds shares in it. The share certificate is the society's paper that says so.

It proves two things: you are a member, and you hold a stated number of shares. It does not replace the sale deed. Ownership of your flat comes from the registered agreement for sale. Our guide to [what a sale deed contains](/blog/what-a-sale-deed-contains) shows what that paper covers.

Check it before you buy. It shows that the society has recorded the seller as a member.

The model bye laws say the society issues the certificate within six months of allotting the shares. Each certificate carries the society's seal and the signatures of its office bearers.

---

## How is a share transferred when a flat is sold?

Rule 24 of the Maharashtra Cooperative Societies Rules, 1961 says a transfer of shares has no effect unless four things are true:

1. It follows the society's bye laws.
2. The society gets a clear 15 days' notice in writing. The notice names the buyer, carries the buyer's consent and membership application, and states the value to be paid.
3. All the seller's dues to the society are cleared.
4. The transfer is entered in the society's books.

Bye laws are the society's own rulebook.

Rule 23 adds a protection for the buyer. A buyer cannot be made to pay more for the share than its value as the rule works it out.

Once the transfer is entered, the society records the buyer as the holder. Agree a date for the endorsed or new certificate with the secretary when you submit the papers.

## What can the society charge on a transfer?

The model bye laws name three separate payments. The transfer fee and the premium are not the same thing.

| Payment | Amount | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer fee | 500 rupees | Seller |
| Premium | Fixed by the general body, up to 25,000 rupees in a municipal corporation area | Seller |
| Entrance fee | 100 rupees | Buyer |

The premium cap comes from the Cooperation Department's order of 9 August 2001 under Section 79A of the Cooperative Societies Act. The Bombay High Court applied it in 2013, holding that a society in a municipal corporation could not charge more than 25,000 rupees. A flat in Borivali, Kandivali or Malad is inside the Mumbai municipal corporation.

Two later rulings point the same way. In a ruling reported in September 2025, the court said a society could not add a "welfare fee" on top. It called that a camouflage attempt to recover more. In February 2026, the court held that State directions fixing such ceilings bind every society.

Our guide to the [society NOC for sale of a flat](/blog/society-noc-for-sale-of-flat-mumbai) covers the family exemption and the NOC itself.

Here is a worked example. The asking figure is an illustration, not a real society's demand.

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Society asks the seller for "transfer charges" | 40,000 rupees |
| Most the bye laws allow: 25,000 premium plus 500 transfer fee | 25,500 rupees |
| Amount above that you can question | 14,500 rupees |

The buyer's 100 rupee entrance fee is separate. If a society adds any other fee, ask which bye law allows it.

## Who pays, the buyer or the seller?

The model bye laws put the transfer fee and the premium on the seller. They put the entrance fee on the buyer. Buyer and seller can still agree to share the cost, so write the answer into the agreement for sale before the price is fixed.

Two other things belong in the same paragraph:

- **The seller's dues.** Rule 24 says the seller's dues to the society must be cleared.
- **The buyer's membership application.** It goes in with the 15 days' notice.

## Can you hold the certificate in joint names?

Yes. Rule 20 lets a society admit joint members. The person named first on the share certificate has the right to vote. All of them carry the liabilities together.

So the order of names on the certificate matters. Decide who is named first before you apply.

## What happens to the certificate after the owner's death?

A member can name a nominee. Under Rule 25 the nomination is a signed document, deposited with the society during the member's lifetime. A statement in the society's own book also counts, if the member signs it and one witness attests it. The member can change it later.

If there is no nominee, the society puts up a public notice at its office inviting claims and objections. The committee then considers them.

For a MHADA flat, the Board has its own process. Our guide to [MHADA transfer on death](/blog/mhada-transfer-on-death) walks through it.

## What if the certificate is lost?

Apply to the committee for a duplicate. The model bye laws say the committee issues a duplicate marked "So Marked". For a lost certificate, attach a copy of the police complaint and an affidavit. For a torn, burnt or soiled one, an affidavit is enough.

Keep a copy of the registered agreement and the society's receipts while the duplicate is processed.

---

## What documents does the society ask for?

Keep these ready when the society records a transfer:

1. The registered agreement for sale
2. The buyer's application for membership and consent
3. The 15 day notice and the seller's dues clearance
4. The seller's application, with the original share certificate
5. Identity and address proof

Once the society has recorded you, the next step is your name in the municipal records. Our guide to [mutation of property](/blog/mutation-of-property) covers that. If the society itself has not yet got its land documents, read [conveyance and deemed conveyance](/blog/conveyance-and-deemed-conveyance).

## Common questions

**What does a society share certificate prove?**
It proves you are a member of the society and hold its shares. It is not the title to the flat. Ownership comes from the registered agreement for sale.

**What is the maximum transfer premium a housing society can charge in Mumbai?**
It is 25,000 rupees in a municipal corporation area, under the order of 9 August 2001. The model bye laws add a 500 rupee transfer fee. The Bombay High Court has held that societies must keep within the ceiling.

**How much notice does a society need for a transfer of shares?**
Rule 24 requires a clear 15 days' notice in writing, with the buyer's name, consent, membership application and the value to be paid.

**Who pays the society transfer fee?**
The model bye laws put the transfer fee and premium on the seller and the 100 rupee entrance fee on the buyer. You can agree to share the cost in the agreement for sale.

**How long does a society take to issue a share certificate?**
The model bye laws say within six months of allotting the shares. On a transfer, agree a date with the secretary when you hand in the papers.

**Can two people hold one share certificate?**
Yes, as joint members. Under Rule 20 the person named first votes, and all carry the liabilities together.

**Can I nominate someone for my flat?**
Yes. Under Rule 25 you sign a nomination and deposit it with the society during your lifetime. A statement in the society's book needs one witness. You can change it later.

**What if my share certificate is lost?**
Apply to the committee for a duplicate, with a copy of the police complaint and an affidavit. The duplicate is marked "So Marked".
