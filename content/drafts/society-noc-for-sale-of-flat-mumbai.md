## The short answer

The Maharashtra model bye laws say a society's NOC is not required to transfer a flat. The seller must still give 15 days' notice and pay the transfer fee. A premium of up to 25,000 rupees applies in a municipal corporation area. **If you or a bank ask for an NOC, the committee considers it within one month.**

---

## Is a society NOC needed to sell a flat in Mumbai?

NOC means no objection certificate. It is a letter from the society saying it has no objection to the sale.

The Registrar's model bye laws for housing societies are clear on this. Bye law 37 says the society's NOC is not required to transfer shares and interest to the buyer. The Housing Manual repeats it. Even so, the seller must give the society 15 days' notice before the transfer.

Buyers and banks often like to see a society letter all the same. If either asks, the seller applies for it. The bye laws let the committee consider the application on merit, within one month.

---

## What can a society refuse?

Less than people expect. The bye laws say the committee must not refuse a transfer or an admission to membership. The only grounds are non compliance with the Act, the Rules, the bye laws, or any other law or government order.

There is also a time limit. If the society does not communicate its decision within three months, the transfer is treated as accepted. The buyer is then deemed to be a member. This comes from section 22(2) of the Maharashtra Cooperative Societies Act.

A transfer made against the Act or the bye laws is void as far as the society is concerned.

---

## How do you apply, and how long does it take?

The steps below follow the model bye laws and the Housing Manual:

1. The seller gives the secretary 15 days' notice, with the buyer's written consent.
2. The secretary puts it before the next committee meeting.
3. The committee decides within 30 days and tells the member within 8 days of deciding.
4. Both sides submit the transfer documents and pay the fees.
5. The society sends the buyer its letter admitting them as a member.

If you want an NOC letter as well, ask in writing. If the society cannot give one, the Housing Manual says it must give you the reasons within 8 days. For a letter wanted for a bank loan, it must reply in writing within 15 days.

---

## What documents does the society ask for?

The transfer bye law lists them. Keep these ready:

- The seller's application for transfer, with the share certificate
- The buyer's application for membership
- The seller's resignation from membership
- The registered agreement, with stamp duty paid
- The reason for the transfer
- The seller's undertaking to clear all dues to the society
- Any NOC needed under another law, or by a lender

## What does the society charge for the transfer and the NOC?

The bye laws name three payments. The premium has a cap.

| Payment | Amount | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer fee | 500 rupees in the model bye laws | Seller |
| Entrance fee | 100 rupees in the model bye laws | Buyer |
| Premium | Set by the society's general body, up to 25,000 rupees in a municipal corporation area | Seller |

The cap comes from the Cooperation Department's order of 9 August 2001 under section 79A. The Bombay High Court relied on it in 2013, holding that a society in a municipal corporation could not charge more than 25,000 rupees. A flat in Borivali, Kandivali or Malad lies in the Mumbai municipal corporation area, so that cap applies to you. A society that sets a lower premium charges the lower one.

On those figures, a seller pays up to 25,500 rupees to the society and a buyer pays 100. The bye laws add that no donation or other fund contribution may be recovered from either side.

Neither document sets a fee for the NOC letter itself, so we do not quote one. If your society names a sum, ask which bye law it comes from.

---

## When is the premium not payable?

The premium does not apply to a transfer to the seller's family, a nominee or an heir after death. It also does not apply to a mutual exchange of flats between members. The Housing Manual says the same.

The bye laws define family to include the husband, wife, father, mother, sister, brother, son and daughter. They also include the son in law, daughter in law, brother in law, sister in law and grandchildren.

---

## Who pays, the buyer or the seller?

The bye laws put the transfer fee and the premium on the seller. They put the entrance fee on the buyer. A buyer and seller can still agree to share the cost. If you do, write it into the agreement, so both sides know who pays what.

## What other NOCs can a sale need?

The transfer bye law asks for any NOC "required under any law" or by a financing agency. Three cases come up in Mumbai:

- **A lender:** where a bank or other financier needs an NOC, the bye laws list it among the transfer papers.
- **MHADA:** a MHADA flat needs the Board's permission as well. Our guide to [MHADA NOC online](/blog/mhada-noc-online) lists the services.
- **The society's own letter:** only if you or the buyer ask for it, as above.

Our guide to [buying a MHADA flat in resale](/blog/how-to-buy-mhada-flat-in-resale) shows how these checks fit together.

## What if the society delays?

Use the time limits. The committee has three months to decide, and silence counts as acceptance. If it refuses, the member can appeal to the Registrar under section 23(2) of the Act. Where the Registrar sends an application to the society, the Housing Manual says it must decide within 60 days. On a dispute over an NOC, the Registrar's decision binds both the society and the member.

Start with a polite written reminder to the secretary, with a copy of your notice and its date.

---

## What should a seller do first?

- Clear every maintenance bill and get a no dues letter from the secretary.
- Keep the share certificate and the original allotment papers together.
- Give the 15 days' notice early, with the buyer's consent.
- Ask the buyer which letter their bank needs.
- Settle who pays the fees, in writing.

## Common questions

**Is a society NOC mandatory to sell a flat in Mumbai?**
No, under the Maharashtra model bye laws. The seller still gives 15 days' notice and follows the transfer steps. A buyer or bank may ask for an NOC, and the committee considers it within one month.

**What is the maximum transfer premium a society can charge in Mumbai?**
It is 25,000 rupees in a municipal corporation area, under the Cooperation Department's order of 9 August 2001. The society's general body fixes the actual figure, which can be lower.

**Who pays the society transfer charges, the buyer or the seller?**
The bye laws put the transfer fee and premium on the seller and the entrance fee on the buyer. You can agree to share the cost, and should write it down.

**How long does the society take to approve a sale?**
The committee decides within 30 days, as the Housing Manual says. If it does not respond within three months, the transfer is deemed accepted.

**Can a society refuse to transfer my flat?**
Only for non compliance with the Act, the Rules, the bye laws, or any other law or government order. If it refuses, you can appeal to the Registrar.

**Does a gift to a family member pay the premium?**
No. The premium is not payable on a transfer to a family member, a nominee or an heir.

**Is the NOC fee fixed by law?**
The model bye laws set the transfer fee and entrance fee, and the Government caps the premium. They do not set a fee for the NOC letter.
