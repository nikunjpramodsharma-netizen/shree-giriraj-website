## The short answer

The Maharashtra model bye laws say a society's NOC is not required to transfer a flat. The seller must still give 15 days' notice and pay the transfer fee. A premium of up to 25,000 rupees applies in a municipal corporation area. **If you or a bank ask for an NOC, the committee considers it within one month.**

---

## Is a society NOC needed to sell a flat in Mumbai?

**No. Bye law 37 of the Maharashtra model bye laws says the society's NOC is not required to transfer a flat's shares to the buyer.** NOC means no objection certificate: a letter from the society saying it has no objection to the sale. The Registrar's Housing Manual says the same.

What the seller does need is notice. Rule 24 of the Maharashtra Cooperative Societies Rules, 1961 asks for a clear 15 days' written notice to the society. The notice carries the buyer's consent and membership application.

---

## Why do buyers and banks still ask for a society NOC?

**Because the letter shows, in one page, that the society knows of the sale and has no dues outstanding against the flat.** A buyer's lender often wants that comfort before it pays out a home loan. The bye laws allow for it: if the seller or the buyer asks for an NOC, the committee considers the application on merit, within one month.

So most sales in Borivali, Kandivali and Malad end up with a society letter, even though the law does not demand one. Asking early keeps the buyer's loan on schedule.

## How do you apply, and how long does it take?

**Give the secretary 15 days' written notice with the buyer's consent; the committee then decides within 30 days.** The steps below follow the model bye laws and the Housing Manual:

1. The seller gives the secretary 15 days' notice, with the buyer's written consent.
2. The secretary puts it before the next committee meeting.
3. The committee decides within 30 days and tells the member within 8 days of deciding.
4. Both sides submit the transfer documents and pay the fees.
5. The society sends the buyer its letter admitting them as a member.

If you want an NOC letter as well, ask in writing. If the society cannot give one, the Housing Manual says it must give you the reasons within 8 days. For a letter wanted for a bank loan, it must reply in writing within 15 days.

## What should the NOC application letter say?

**A short letter to the secretary that gives notice of the sale and asks for the NOC is enough.** Many societies have their own printed forms, so ask the secretary first. If yours does not, this plain format covers what Rule 24 and the bye laws ask for:

> To the Secretary, ___ Co operative Housing Society Ltd, ___ (address).
> Subject: Notice of transfer of Flat No ___ and request for a no objection certificate.
> I, ___, hold share certificate No ___ for Flat No ___. I give 15 days' notice that I intend to transfer my shares and interest in the flat to ___, for ___ rupees. The buyer's written consent and application for membership are enclosed. I undertake to clear all my dues to the society before the transfer. Please issue a no objection certificate for the sale, and for the buyer's home loan from ___ Bank. Yours faithfully, ___ (name, date, signature).

Attach a copy of the share certificate and the latest maintenance receipt. Keep a copy stamped "received" by the society office, because its date starts the 15 days.

## What documents does the society ask for?

**Bye law 37 lists them, and the share certificate goes in first.** Keep these ready:

- The seller's application for transfer, with the share certificate
- The buyer's application for membership
- The seller's resignation from membership
- The registered agreement, with stamp duty paid
- The reason for the transfer
- The seller's undertaking to clear all dues to the society
- Any NOC needed under another law, or by a lender

## What does the society charge for the transfer and the NOC?

**At most 25,500 rupees from the seller in Mumbai: a 500 rupee transfer fee plus a premium capped at 25,000 rupees.** The buyer pays a 100 rupee entrance fee. These are the three payments the bye laws name:

| Payment | Amount | Who pays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer fee | 500 rupees in the model bye laws | Seller |
| Entrance fee | 100 rupees in the model bye laws | Buyer |
| Premium | Set by the society's general body, up to 25,000 rupees in a municipal corporation area | Seller |

The cap comes from the Cooperation Department's order of 9 August 2001 under section 79A. The Bombay High Court relied on it in 2013, holding that a society in a municipal corporation could not charge more than 25,000 rupees. A flat in Borivali, Kandivali or Malad lies in the Mumbai municipal corporation area, so that cap applies to you. A society that sets a lower premium charges the lower one.

Here is a worked example. The asking figure is an illustration, not a real society's demand.

| Item | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| Society asks the seller for "transfer charges" | 40,000 rupees |
| Most the bye laws allow: 25,000 premium plus 500 transfer fee | 25,500 rupees |
| Amount above that you can question | 14,500 rupees |

Bye law 37 adds that no donation or other fund contribution may be recovered from either side. In September 2025 the Bombay High Court also refused a "welfare fee" added on top of the transfer fee. Neither the bye laws nor the Manual sets a fee for the NOC letter itself, so we do not quote one. If your society names a sum, ask which bye law it comes from.

## When is the premium not payable?

**On a transfer within the family, to a nominee or to an heir, and on a swap of flats between members.** The Housing Manual says the same.

The bye laws define family to include the husband, wife, father, mother, sister, brother, son and daughter. They also include the son in law, daughter in law, brother in law, sister in law and grandchildren.

---

## Who pays society NOC charges, the buyer or the seller?

**The seller pays the transfer fee and the premium; the buyer pays the 100 rupee entrance fee.** That is how the bye laws split it. A buyer and seller can still agree to share the cost. If you do, write it into the agreement, so both sides know who pays what.

---

## What other NOCs can a sale need?

**Only those another law or a lender requires.** The transfer bye law asks for any NOC "required under any law" or by a financing agency. Three cases come up in Mumbai:

- **A lender:** where a bank or other financier needs an NOC, the bye laws list it among the transfer papers.
- **MHADA:** a MHADA flat needs the Board's permission as well. Our guide to [MHADA NOC online](/blog/mhada-noc-online) lists the services.
- **The society's own letter:** only if you or the buyer ask for it, as above.

## What happens if the society does not give the NOC?

**The transfer can still go ahead: if the society does not decide within three months, the buyer is deemed a member.** This comes from section 22(2) of the Maharashtra Cooperative Societies Act. The committee may refuse a transfer only for non compliance with the Act, the Rules, the bye laws, or another law or government order.

If it refuses, the member can appeal to the Registrar under section 23(2) of the Act. Where the Registrar sends an application to the society, the Housing Manual says it must decide within 60 days. On a dispute over an NOC, the Registrar's decision binds both the society and the member.

Start with a polite written reminder to the secretary, with a copy of your notice and its date. Most delays end there.

## What should a seller do first?

**Clear the dues and put the papers in one folder before a buyer is found.**

- Clear every maintenance bill and get a no dues letter from the secretary.
- Keep the share certificate and the original purchase papers together.
- Give the 15 days' notice early, with the buyer's consent.
- Ask the buyer which letter their bank needs.
- Settle who pays the fees, in writing.

A seller who has these ready can sign the agreement the week a buyer says yes.

## Common questions

**Is a society NOC mandatory to sell a flat in Mumbai?**
No, under the Maharashtra model bye laws. The seller still gives 15 days' notice and follows the transfer steps. A buyer or bank may ask for an NOC, and the committee considers it within one month.

**Why is a society NOC required if the law does not demand it?**
Buyers and their banks ask for it as comfort that the society knows of the sale and has no dues against the flat. The bye laws let the committee issue one on request, within one month.

**What is the format of a society NOC application for sale of a flat?**
A letter to the secretary giving 15 days' notice of the transfer, naming the buyer and the price. Enclose the buyer's consent and membership application, and ask for the NOC.

**What is the maximum transfer premium a society can charge in Mumbai?**
It is 25,000 rupees in a municipal corporation area, under the order of 9 August 2001. The society's general body fixes the actual figure, which can be lower.

**Who pays the society transfer charges, the buyer or the seller?**
The seller pays the transfer fee and the premium. The buyer pays the 100 rupee entrance fee. You can agree to share the cost, and should write it down.

**How long does the society take to approve a sale?**
The committee decides within 30 days, as the Housing Manual says. If it does not respond within three months, the transfer is deemed accepted.

**What happens if the society does not give an NOC?**
The sale can still proceed, because the bye laws say no NOC is required for the transfer. If the society refuses the transfer itself, you can appeal to the Registrar.

**Does a gift to a family member pay the premium?**
No. The premium is not payable on a transfer to a family member, a nominee or an heir.

**Is the NOC fee fixed by law?**
No. The model bye laws set the transfer fee and entrance fee, and the Government caps the premium. They set no fee for the NOC letter.
