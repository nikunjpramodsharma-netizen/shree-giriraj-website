## Step 1: How much money do I need to buy a flat in Mumbai?

You need the down payment, plus stamp duty and registration, plus moving and fit out costs. Banks do not lend against the last three.

The Reserve Bank of India, or RBI, sets how much a bank may lend against a flat. This is the loan to value ratio, which simply means the loan as a share of the flat's value. Paragraph 111 of the RBI's credit facilities directions, updated on 1 October 2026, gives two bands that matter for most Mumbai flats.

- A loan above 30 lakh rupees and up to 75 lakh: up to 80 percent of the value.
- A loan above 75 lakh rupees: up to 75 percent of the value.

Paragraph 113 says a bank must not add stamp duty, registration and documentation charges to the cost it finances. The only exception is a home costing 10 lakh rupees or less. So you pay those charges from your own pocket.

Here is a worked example for a flat priced at 1.2 crore rupees.

| Item | Amount in rupees |
| --- | --- |
| Flat price | 1,20,00,000 |
| Most a bank may lend (75 percent) | 90,00,000 |
| Your down payment (25 percent) | 30,00,000 |
| Stamp duty (6 percent) and registration (30,000), man or joint buyers | 7,50,000 |
| **Cash you need on the table** | **37,50,000** |
| Same, woman buying in her sole name (5 percent duty) | 36,30,000 |

## Step 2: How does a home loan sanction work?

Get the loan sanctioned before you choose a flat. A sanction letter is the bank's written promise of how much it will lend you. The bank gives it after checking your income and your credit score.

Two words are easy to mix up:

- **Sanction** is the bank saying yes to an amount.
- **Disbursement** is the bank paying the seller, in stages or in full.

The sanction comes first. Disbursement comes only after the bank has also checked the flat's papers.

Square Yards reported in June 2026 that most banks cap your total monthly instalments at 50 to 55 percent of net monthly pay. The monthly instalment is called the EMI. Each bank sets its own rule, so ask yours.

For the rate, take a named example. State Bank of India publishes its home loan rate as starting from 7.50 percent a year, with effect from 15 October 2026. Business Today reported its range for regular home loans as 7.50 to 8.80 percent. Your own rate depends on your profile. The [home loan EMI calculator](/tools/home-loan-emi-calculator) shows the instalment at any rate. For tax on a home loan, please ask your chartered accountant.

## Step 3: Should my first flat be ready, resale or under construction?

Ready and resale flats let you see what you buy and get the keys at registration. An under construction flat often asks less per square foot and lets you pay in stages. In return, you wait for possession on a promised date.

For a first flat we often suggest a resale or ready flat, because you see the building and the society on day one. Our [under construction or ready to move guide](/blog/under-construction-vs-ready-to-move-flat) sets out the trade.

## Step 4: What should I check when I visit a flat?

Visit at the hour you would be at home.

- **Water.** Ask the timing and check the pressure on the floor you are buying.
- **Parking.** Ask what the society will allot in writing.
- **The building.** Look at the staircase, the lift and the terrace.
- **Carpet area.** Ask for it as RERA defines it: the usable floor inside the walls, under section 2(k). RERA is the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act 2016. Our [carpet area guide](/blog/carpet-area-vs-built-up-area) and [carpet area calculator](/tools/carpet-area-calculator) show how the rate per square foot changes with it.

## Step 6: Is the token amount refundable, and what is an agreement for sale?

A token is a small part payment that holds the flat for you. Put its terms on a written receipt: the amount, the flat, the date, and what happens if either side steps back. We help you write that receipt. With the terms in writing, the token is easy to settle either way.

For a new project, RERA section 13 sets a ceiling. A promoter may not take more than 10 percent of the price as an advance before signing and registering a written agreement for sale. That agreement must state the payment dates and the possession date. A promoter is the developer of the project.

The **agreement for sale** is the contract that says what is sold, at what price and by when. The **sale deed** is the final document that transfers ownership. Our [sale deed guide](/blog/what-a-sale-deed-contains) shows what goes in it.

## Step 7: What are stamp duty and registration, and who pays?

The buyer pays both. Stamp duty is the state's tax on the transfer. Registration is the fee for putting it on the public record.

In Mumbai the duty is 6 percent for a man, or a man and a woman buying jointly. That is a 5 percent base plus a 1 percent metro cess. It is 5 percent for a woman buying in her sole name. Registration is 1 percent, capped at 30,000 rupees. Both are charged on the higher of the agreed price and the government's ready reckoner value. The [ready reckoner guide](/blog/ready-reckoner-rate-mumbai) explains how to find the value for your zone.

## Step 8: How do payment and possession work?

For a resale flat, you pay in the order the agreement sets. Your bank pays its share as its sanction terms say. Then the seller hands over the keys. Take the possession letter, the society's receipt for dues and every original paper.

For a new project, you pay in stages as construction moves. Under RERA section 4(2)(l)(D), the promoter deposits 70 percent of buyers' money in a separate project account. The possession date in your agreement should match the MahaRERA page.

## Step 9: What happens after I move in?

1. **Society transfer.** Apply for the share certificate in your name. Read [what the share certificate does and does not prove](/blog/society-share-certificate-transfer-fee).

## Common questions

**How much money do I need to buy a flat in Mumbai?**
On a 1.2 crore rupee flat in Mumbai you need about 37.5 lakh rupees of your own money. That is a 30 lakh down payment plus 7.5 lakh of stamp duty and registration. That assumes a bank lends 90 lakh, the RBI's 75 percent limit. A woman buying alone needs about 36.3 lakh.

**How much down payment do I need for a home loan?**
You need at least 20 percent down on a home loan of 30 to 75 lakh rupees, and 25 percent above that. The bands are in paragraph 111 of the RBI's credit facilities directions. Stamp duty and registration are not financed, so pay them in addition.

**Which documents does a first time buyer need?**
A first time buyer needs identity and address proof, income proof and bank statements for the loan. For the flat, check the past sale deeds, the encumbrance search, the occupancy certificate and the society papers. For a new project, check the MahaRERA registration.

**Should my first flat be resale or new?**
A resale flat suits most first buyers because you see the building and take the keys at registration. A new project often asks less per square foot and lets you pay in stages, but you wait for possession.

**Is the token amount refundable?**
A token is refundable only on the terms written on its receipt, so agree them before you pay. For a new project, RERA section 13 stops a promoter taking more than 10 percent of the price before a registered agreement for sale.

**What is the first thing to do when buying a flat?**
The first step in buying a flat is to get a home loan sanction letter, which tells you how much a bank will lend. Add your savings to it, then take off stamp duty, registration and brokerage. What is left is your price ceiling.

**Can I buy a flat on a 70,000 rupee salary?**
A take home pay of 70,000 rupees can support a home loan of about 43 lakh rupees. That assumes a bank allows half of it for the EMI. That is an EMI of 35,000 at SBI's starting rate of 7.50 percent over 20 years. Other EMIs reduce it.

**Do I need a lawyer to buy a flat?**
Every flat's papers should be read by someone who knows them before you pay a token. We read the title, the society papers and the registered record first. For a complex title, a property lawyer's opinion is worth having.

**What is the difference between an agreement for sale and a sale deed?**
An agreement for sale is the contract signed before a sale is complete, while a sale deed is the final document that transfers ownership. The agreement should state the payment dates and the possession date. Our sale deed guide shows what goes in the deed.
