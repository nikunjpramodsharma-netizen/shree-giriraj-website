## The short answer: renting costs less each month, buying builds a home you own

Rent or buy comes down to three numbers. They are the price against the rent, the loan rate and the years you stay.

Here is one flat worked through in rupees. It is a 1 BHK in Kandivali West with 450 square feet of carpet area. Carpet area is the usable floor inside the walls. That is the top of the 400 to 450 square feet our [resale flats page](/services/resale-flats) uses for a 1 BHK.

Square Yards puts Kandivali West apartments at 27,200 rupees a square foot in October 2026. So the flat asks about 1.22 crore rupees. NoBroker's October 2026 asking rents for a 1 BHK there run 18,000 to 35,000 a month. The midpoint is 26,500.

- **Owning** costs about 77,950 rupees a month and takes 38.2 lakh rupees of your cash.
- **Renting** costs about 26,500 rupees a month, plus a refundable deposit of about 53,000 to 80,000 rupees.

Renting costs less each month. Buying gives you a flat that is yours, and part of every EMI builds your share of it. The rest of this page shows how the two compare, and what makes buying come out ahead.

## What does it cost to buy? The cash on day one

Banks lend against the flat's value, but not all of it. The Reserve Bank of India sets the limits in paragraph 111 of its Credit Facilities Directions 2025, updated on 1 October 2026. LTV, or loan to value, is the share of the price a bank may lend. On a loan above 75 lakh rupees, the LTV is at most 75 percent.

On a 1.22 crore flat, that means a loan of up to 91.8 lakh rupees. You bring the other 25 percent. Under paragraph 113, the bank cannot count stamp duty and registration in the value it lends against. So those come from your own money too.

| Item | Buying the 1.22 crore flat |
| --- | --- |
| Loan at 75 percent | 91,80,000 |
| Your share of the price | 30,60,000 |
| Stamp duty, 5 percent plus 1 percent metro cess | 7,34,400 |
| Registration, 1 percent capped at 30,000 | 30,000 |
| **Cash needed on day one** | **38,24,400** |

Stamp duty is the state's tax on the sale deed. The metro cess is an extra 1 percent levy on Mumbai sales, linked to the metro projects. Registration is the fee to record the deed. The 6 percent total is for a man, or a man and a woman together. A woman buying alone pays 1 percent less.

Our [stamp duty guide](/blog/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-mumbai) has every case, and the [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator-mumbai) does your own sum. Brokerage and furnishing come on top.

Renting the same flat needs a refundable deposit. Two to three months' rent is normal in newer societies, so 53,000 to 79,500 rupees. The [deposit guide](/blog/rent-deposit-months-western-suburbs) explains how to get it back on time.

## How does the monthly outgo compare?

State Bank of India publishes its home loan rate as 7.50 percent a year onwards, with effect from 15 October 2026. Business Today reported its regular home loan range as 7.50 to 8.80 percent (18 October 2026). We use 7.50 percent over 20 years. Your own rate depends on your bank and your profile.

| Monthly | Buy | Rent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EMI on 91.8 lakh at 7.50 percent, 20 years | 73,953 | 0 |
| Society charges (our assumption) | 4,000 | Usually the landlord's |
| Rent | 0 | 26,500 |
| **Total** | **77,953** | **26,500** |

So owning costs about 51,450 rupees a month more. An EMI is the equal monthly instalment that repays the loan with interest. At 8.80 percent, the top of the reported range, it is 81,418. Society charges are the monthly maintenance the housing society collects.

In the first year, about 6.8 lakh rupees of the EMIs is interest. About 2.1 lakh rupees repays the loan and becomes part of the flat you own. Early EMIs are mostly interest, because the balance is at its largest.

## When does buying win? The break even, in plain words

Owning has costs that renting does not. They are stamp duty, registration, loan interest and society charges. Renting has one cost, the rent. Your down payment and the loan you repay are not costs in this sense. They become the flat you own.

So the break even is simple. The flat must gain in value by at least the extra you spent to own it. Here is that sum for the Kandivali flat. It is an illustration of the target, not a forecast.

| After | Duty, registration, interest and society charges | Rent over the same time | Price gain needed to break even | As a share of 1.22 crore |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 years | 42.4 lakh | 15.9 lakh | about 26.5 lakh | about 22 percent |
| 10 years | 71.7 lakh | 31.8 lakh | about 39.9 lakh | about 33 percent |

The table holds rent at 26,500 rupees every year, and rising rents would narrow the gap. It leaves out what your 38.2 lakh could earn elsewhere and the cost of selling. Those two push the target further out.

We do not forecast prices. The table gives you a rupee target instead. Then you can judge whether the flat can reach it in the years you plan to stay.

## When does renting suit you best?

Renting tends to suit you when:

- You may move cities or jobs within five years.
- The 38.2 lakh rupees is most of your savings, and you would rather keep it free to use.
- You want your income to grow before you take on an EMI.
- You are still choosing the pocket and the society that suit you.

## When does buying suit you best?

Buying tends to suit you when:

- You plan to stay many years, ten or more.
- You have the cash and a steady income that carries the EMI comfortably.
- You value a home that is yours to keep, renovate and pass on.
- The price to rent ratio is lower, as in Malad East's 30 years of rent above.

SBI's regular home loans are linked to its external benchmark rate, so the EMI can move when that rate changes. Your home stays yours either way, with no rent rise or notice to vacate.

## What does a rent vs buy calculator leave out?

Most rent vs buy calculators miss these:

- **Society charges and repairs.** They are paid monthly and rise over time.
- **Brokerage and furnishing**, paid once.
- **Property tax.** Homes of up to 500 square feet carpet area pay no BMC property tax since 1 January 2022. So a 450 square foot 1 BHK pays none. Our [BMC property tax guide](/blog/bmc-property-tax) explains the rule.
- **Selling costs** when you move on.

Tax benefits on a home loan are outside this comparison. A chartered accountant can tell you what applies to you.

## Common questions

**Is it better to rent or buy in Mumbai?**
Renting costs less every month, and buying builds a home you own. On a 1.22 crore rupee 1 BHK in Kandivali West, owning costs about 51,450 rupees a month more than renting. That uses SBI's 7.50 percent starting rate. Buying comes out ahead when you stay many years and the flat gains enough in value.

**How long must I stay for buying to win?**
There is no fixed number of years, because the break even depends on how much the flat gains. On our Kandivali example, the flat must gain about 26.5 lakh rupees in five years, or 39.9 lakh in ten. The longer you stay, the more the one time stamp duty is spread out.

**Why are Mumbai rents low against prices?**
A 1 BHK costs about 30 to 38 years of rent in Borivali West, Kandivali West and Malad East. That is on 2026 asking figures from Square Yards and NoBroker. Rent reflects what a tenant pays for a home this month. The price reflects what a buyer pays to own it for good.

**What price to rent ratio is good?**
There is no official benchmark for the price to rent ratio. It is the price divided by a year's rent, and our example flats sit at 30 to 38. A lower number leans towards buying and a higher one towards renting, other things equal. Look at the monthly gap too.

**Is rent a waste of money?**
No, rent pays for a home today with no loan, no stamp duty and no repairs. An EMI also goes mostly to interest in the early years. In year one of our example, about 6.8 lakh of the 8.9 lakh paid in EMIs is interest. Both are the cost of having a home.

**Can I rent and invest the down payment instead?**
Yes, and it is the fairest way to compare the two. The 38.2 lakh rupees you would put into the flat could stay invested while you rent. We do not quote returns, because they depend on where you invest. Set your adviser's answer beside the 51,450 rupees a month it costs to own.

**Should I rent first before buying a flat?**
Renting in the suburb for a year shows you the society, the water timing and the commute before you commit. That helps if you are still choosing a pocket. If you already know it well, buying sooner can make sense. We arrange viewings for either.

**Does the sum change for a 2 BHK?**
The sum scales with the price, so a 2 BHK raises both sides. It costs more to buy and more to rent, and the monthly gap in rupees grows too. Our [rental yield guide](/blog/rental-yield-mumbai) compares the two sizes for a landlord.

**Is a rent vs buy calculator accurate?**
A rent vs buy calculator is only as accurate as its inputs: the rate, the rent, the price and the society charges. Check each one against the flat you are considering. Our [home loan EMI calculator](/tools/home-loan-emi-calculator) works out your EMI, and you can set the rent beside it.

**What if I may move cities?**
If you may move within a few years, renting keeps you flexible. Selling a flat takes time and has costs at both ends. If you buy and then move, the flat can be let out. Our [investment advisory service](/services/investment-advisory) can work through that with you.
