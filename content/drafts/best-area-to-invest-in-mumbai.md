## Why a pocket matters more than a suburb

Borivali is the clearest example. It splits into the station belt, which is older, denser and better connected, and the Link Road side, which is newer and quieter. The two can sit at similar rates and behave differently: different tenants, different resale.

So the useful exercise is a checklist you can run on a pocket, and then on a building inside it.

---

## The six checks

### 1. Rent against price

What would this flat, in this building, rent for this month, against what it costs to buy this month? Everything else on this list supports that number. Run it through the [rental yield calculator](/tools/rental-yield-calculator) on the net basis.

### 2. Who the tenant pool is

Every pocket has a typical tenant:

- Families near schools
- Single professionals near the station
- Office workers near the business parks

That tenant decides how fast a flat lets and what rent it gets. It also decides what the flat should be. A pocket that lets to families rewards a second bedroom.

### 3. How easy resale is

How long do flats in this building take to sell, and who buys them? Some sell in weeks. Others sit for a year because of a conveyance problem or a reputation. A modest yield in a building people want can beat a high yield in one they do not.

### 4. The building stock

Check these about the building: its age and condition, whether the society runs well, and whether conveyance is done. Check the water pressure on upper floors and the parking the society will actually allot. These decide what the flat is worth to the next buyer, and none shows on a listing.

Conveyance means the society holds the legal title to its land. Read [what conveyance and deemed conveyance mean](/blog/conveyance-and-deemed-conveyance) before buying into a society that has not completed it.

### 5. Infrastructure that is committed

Count what is funded, tendered and under construction. Treat a line on a map that is only announced as a possibility, and do not pay for it today.

### 6. New supply

Several large projects finishing in one pocket put a lot of rental stock on the market at once. That can affect your rent in the year they hand over. You can check it: MahaRERA registration is public, and every project carries its declared completion date. Our comparison of [under construction and ready flats](/blog/under-construction-vs-ready-to-move-flat) explains how to read that page.

## Common questions

**Is real estate a good investment in Mumbai?**
It can be. A flat here returns rent while you hold it and whatever the market gives when you sell. The rent is measurable before you buy. The sale price is not. The outcome usually turns on the specific building, the pocket and the price you paid, more than on the city.

**Which is better for investment, Borivali or Kandivali?**
Each has strong pockets, and the spread inside each is larger than the difference between them. Compare two specific pockets or buildings using the table and the six checks. Borivali is an owner occupier suburb with steady rents. Kandivali has two metro lines and the widest choice of new homes.

**Should I buy under construction or ready possession?**
They carry different risks. A ready flat earns rent at once and you see what you buy. An under construction flat ties up money with no income until handover and carries delivery risk. The MahaRERA registration and the developer's record matter more than the brochure.

**How much should I invest in property in Mumbai?**
That depends on your finances more than on property. Property is illiquid and the transaction costs are high, so it suits money you will not need back quickly. An adviser who sees your full position can help you size it.

**Does a MahaRERA number mean a project is safe?**
It means the project is registered and its declared details, including the completion date, are on the public record. That is a fact you can check rather than a guarantee, and it is still worth checking on anything under construction.
