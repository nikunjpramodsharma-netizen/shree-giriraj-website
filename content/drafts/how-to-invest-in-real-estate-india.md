## Start with the job your money has to do

Most people who come to us have already picked a suburb and ask about a flat. A better first question is what the money must do.

- **Pay you every month?** Then you are buying rent. The tenant matters more than the tower.
- **Grow quietly for ten years?** Then you are buying a pocket and a building's future. Rent is a bonus.
- **Possibly needed in three years?** Then property may not suit you. Selling a flat takes months and costs money at both ends.

Answer that honestly and the rest of this guide becomes short.

A REIT is a real estate investment trust. It is listed on the stock exchange and pays out most of its rent. An SM REIT is a small and medium version, regulated by SEBI since 2024.

The rest of this guide covers the direct route, a flat or shop in the three suburbs we work in. That is where a local agency can help most.

## What it costs to get in: a 1.2 crore rupee flat in Borivali

Here is the upfront cost on a flat priced at 1.2 crore rupees. We worked it out with the stamp duty rules on our [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator-mumbai).

| Item | Man, or man and woman jointly | Woman in sole name |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agreement price | 1,20,00,000 | 1,20,00,000 |
| Stamp duty, base rate | 6,00,000 (5 percent) | 4,80,000 (4 percent) |
| Metro cess | 1,20,000 (1 percent) | 1,20,000 (1 percent) |
| Registration | 30,000 (capped) | 30,000 (capped) |
| **Duty and registration** | **7,50,000** | **6,30,000** |
| Price plus duty and registration | 1,27,50,000 | 1,26,30,000 |

Things to know about this table:

- Duty is charged on the higher of the agreed price and the government's ready reckoner value. The table assumes the price is the higher one.
- Registration is 1 percent of the value, capped at 30,000 rupees.
- Joint ownership by a man and a woman pays the full rate. The women's concession applies only when women are the only buyers.
- Brokerage is agreed in writing before anything starts. Fit out depends on the flat. Neither is in the table.

Add everything that goes in and you have the true capital. The rent has to earn a return on that figure, not the sticker price.

## What a flat returns

A flat returns two things, and it helps to keep them apart.

**Rent while you hold it.** The honest measure is net yield. Take a year of the rent you will actually collect. Subtract society charges, property tax, repairs and a month of vacancy. Divide that by everything you paid, including duty, registration, brokerage and fit out. The [rental yield article](/blog/rental-yield-mumbai) walks through the sum and shows 2026 rents for the three suburbs. The [calculator](/tools/rental-yield-calculator) does it for any flat.

**What the market gives when you sell.** Nobody can promise this, and we do not try. We can tell you what decides it. The pocket, the building's age and society, its redevelopment position, and the number of similar flats for sale at once all matter. All of those can be checked today.

## Choosing the flat: a seven step checklist

1. **Who is the tenant?** Name the person before you name the building. A family near a school and station wants a different flat from a young professional near Mindspace.
2. **Will the society register a tenant easily?** Some older societies have views on tenants. We ask before the first visit.
3. **What does it let for?** The achieved rent, not the listing price. We know because we let flats in these buildings.
4. **What are the outgoings?** Ask for the last twelve months of society and tax bills.
5. **Where is redevelopment?** A society close to redevelopment is a different purchase from one that has been "next year" for a decade.
6. **Is the paper clean?** Title chain, share certificate, conveyance, occupancy certificate and no encumbrance. Our [paperwork guides](/guides) explain each one.
7. **How many similar flats will be for sale when you want out?** Several large projects finishing together put a lot of stock on the market at once.

## Under construction or ready?

Under construction costs less per square foot in the same project and lets you pay in stages. It earns nothing until possession and carries delivery risk. A ready flat earns rent from the first month and you see what you buy, at a higher price.

Since 2017, the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, or RERA, has made the delivery risk easier to read. Every project is registered with a public possession date. Seventy percent of buyers' money must sit in the project's own construction account. Structural defects are the developer's to fix for five years after possession.

Our [side by side comparison](/blog/under-construction-vs-ready-to-move-flat) sets out the differences, and our [new project bookings service](/services/new-project-bookings) reads the RERA page with you.

## Common questions

**Is real estate a good investment in India?**
It can be, and the western suburbs have rewarded patient owners for decades. It is illiquid, the entry costs are real, and the return depends on the specific building more than on the city. Judge the flat in front of you on its rent, its outgoings and its exit.

**How much money do you need to start investing in property?**
For a flat, the down payment plus duty, registration and brokerage. On a 1.2 crore rupee flat, duty and registration alone are 6.3 to 7.5 lakh rupees. For a listed REIT, the price of one unit through a demat account. For an SM REIT, ten lakh rupees.

**Should a first investor buy a 1 BHK or a 2 BHK?**
A 1 BHK usually lets faster and shows a higher gross yield. A 2 BHK attracts families who stay longer and resells to a wider pool. Near Mindspace the 1 BHK often wins. In Thakur Village and Borivali the 2 BHK usually does.

**Is it better to invest in Mumbai or a smaller city?**
Smaller cities can show higher gross yields on paper. Mumbai shows deeper demand and an easier exit. We advise only on the three suburbs we know, so we would rather tell you exactly what a flat here earns.

**Do I need a property agency to invest?**
You need someone who has been inside the building. Portals show you every listing. A local agency adds the water timing, the society's attitude to tenants and the real achieved rent, none of which is on a listing.
