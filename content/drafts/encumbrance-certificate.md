## The short answer

An encumbrance certificate lists the transactions registered against a property over a stated period. It is used to show that a property is free of registered mortgages, liens or disputes. **A lender will usually ask for one before sanctioning a home loan**, and a careful buyer asks for one before paying a token.

---

## What it is actually telling you

It is a search of the register for one property over a date range. What comes back is a list of what was registered against it: sales, mortgages, gifts, court attachments.

It is a record of what is **registered**. That is a meaningful distinction and it is where people get a false sense of safety. An unregistered arrangement will not appear. Neither will a dispute that has not reached the register.

## What it does not cover

- Anything that was never registered
- Tax or society dues, which sit elsewhere
- Physical condition, encroachment or possession disputes
- Whether the seller is who they say they are

**Does a clear certificate mean the title is clear?**
