## How many months of deposit is normal in Borivali, Kandivali and Malad?

**Two to three months' rent in a newer society, and more in some older buildings and premium addresses.** These are the going figures for this belt, from the portal guides listed below, read on 16 November 2026. The last column assumes a rent of 40,000 rupees a month, as an example.

## Is there a legal limit on the rent deposit in Maharashtra?

**No. No Maharashtra law sets the size of a deposit.** The Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 explains how a leave and licence agreement must be made. It says nothing about the deposit amount. A leave and licence agreement is the Mumbai form of rent agreement, usually for eleven months.

The main rule is section 55. Every such agreement must be in writing and registered, and the landlord must register it. If it is not registered, a court goes by the tenant's account of the terms unless the landlord can prove otherwise.

The two month figure you see online comes from the Model Tenancy Act, 2021. That is a template the central government wrote for states to adopt. We found no Maharashtra notification adopting it. The state's Rent Control (Amendment) Bill of 23 July 2025 dealt with kitchens and structural alterations, not deposits.

So a landlord who asks for three months is within the law. A tenant who quotes the two month cap is quoting a law that does not apply here. The deposit is simply a term you agree and write down.

One more number is worth knowing. A NoBroker study reported by Business Standard on 16 July 2026 put Mumbai tenants' deposits at 41,156 crore rupees. That was the most of the six metros studied. That money earns the tenant nothing. Every month of deposit you negotiate off stays in your own account.

## When is the deposit negotiable?

**When you lower the landlord's risk: a registered agreement, steady income and a likely long stay.** A deposit is the landlord's cover against three things: rent that stops, society dues left unpaid, and damage beyond ordinary wear. A tenant who lowers those three risks has room to negotiate.

What helps you:

- A registered agreement rather than a handshake
- A salary slip or an employer letter
- Paying society dues directly
- A tenancy that looks likely to last
- A flat that has sat empty for a while, because a month of vacancy costs the landlord more than a month of deposit protects

What makes a landlord firmer:

- A short stay or a first job
- An owner who has had a bad experience before
- A building whose society has strict rules on tenants

If a landlord in that position prefers the full deposit, the next flat often has more room to move.

The easiest trade to ask for is time rather than money. Ask whether the deposit can be paid in two parts, one at signing and one at possession. You can also ask whether a slightly higher rent can stand in for a lower lump sum. Do the sum first. An extra 1,000 rupees a month over eleven months is 11,000 rupees you will not see again.

## What does the deposit cover?

**Unpaid rent, unpaid society dues, and damage beyond normal wear and tear.** The deposit covers unpaid rent, unpaid society dues, and damage beyond normal wear and tear. Wear and tear means the ordinary ageing of a home that people have lived in.

Section 108(m) of the Transfer of Property Act lets a landlord recover for damage the tenant causes. It does not cover ordinary deterioration. Courts treat faded paint, small scuffs and ageing fittings as ordinary.

Painting is where most handover arguments start. Say a flat shows only a year of normal living. A landlord who deducts the full cost of repainting it is charging wear and tear as if it were damage. A hole in the wall, a broken fitting or a burn on the counter is damage, and a deduction for it is fair.

## How do you get the deposit back?

**Put the return date in the agreement, photograph the flat on day one and take the refund by bank transfer.** These steps work in every society:

1. Put the amount, the permitted deductions and the return date in the written agreement. Do not leave them to a verbal promise or to "as per practice".
2. Photograph every room on the day you move in, including anything already damaged. Send the photographs to the landlord the same day so they carry a date.
3. Clear the society dues and the electricity bill before you hand over, and keep the receipts.
4. Ask for an itemised list of any deductions. One line saying "cleaning and painting" with no receipts is the usual start of a dispute.
5. Take the refund by bank transfer, never in cash.

Courts and rent authorities generally treat fifteen to thirty days from handing over vacant possession as reasonable. Write that number into the agreement, so the refund runs on a date both sides signed.

If the money does not come back, you have these routes in Maharashtra:

- The Court of Small Causes, Mumbai's city court for money claims of modest size
- A civil suit for recovery
- The district consumer commission, where the tenancy was arranged through a platform or broker

A registered agreement with a deposit clause makes any of these straightforward. An unregistered agreement does not hurt the tenant, because section 55 says the tenant's version of the terms prevails.

Since 31 July 2026, the 15 zonal Deputy Commissioners of Police in Mumbai act as Competent Authority under the Maharashtra Rent Control Act. A Mumbai Police press note of 12 November 2026 shows them ordering an overstaying licensee out. In that order, the tenant's deposit claim was left to be pursued separately. So the deposit clause in your agreement remains your main protection.

## Which four papers keep your deposit safe?

**A payment record, a registered agreement, a deposit clause and day one photographs.** A smooth refund usually comes down to these four documents. Check that you have all four before you pay:

- A receipt for the money, or a bank transfer record
- A registered agreement
- A deposit clause that lists the deductions
- Photographs from day one

If one is missing, fix it before the money moves. With all four in place, the refund is a formality.

## Common questions

**How many months of deposit is normal in Borivali, Kandivali and Malad?**
Two to three months in newer societies, as Mittiyo's July 2026 guide puts it. Some older buildings and premium addresses ask more, historically up to five or six months.

**Can a landlord ask for more than ten months?**
Legally yes, because Maharashtra sets no cap. In practice ten months is three to four times the going rate in this belt. It is a good reason to look at another flat.

**Is the deposit refundable in full?**
Yes, less itemised deductions for unpaid rent, unpaid dues and damage beyond ordinary wear and tear. Painting after normal use is wear and tear. Ask for the list, with receipts.

**When is the deposit returned?**
On the date the agreement states. If it is silent, fifteen to thirty days from the day you hand back the keys is what courts treat as reasonable. Write that number in.

**Is there a legal limit on the security deposit in Maharashtra?**
No. The Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 sets no cap. The two month limit often quoted is in the central Model Tenancy Act, 2021, which we found no Maharashtra law adopting. The deposit is whatever the registered agreement says.

**Did the new rent rules cap deposits at two months in Mumbai?**
The reports of new rent rules from December 2025 describe the Model Tenancy Act, 2021, a central model for states to adopt. As of 16 November 2026 we found no Maharashtra law enacting it, so the Rent Control Act still applies.

**Is an 11 month rent agreement valid in Maharashtra?**
Yes, once it is registered. Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act requires every leave and licence to be in writing and registered, whatever its length.

**What is the stamp duty on a rent agreement in Mumbai?**
It is 0.25 percent of the rent for the term, plus 10 percent a year on the refundable deposit. Registration adds 1,000 rupees. Our [rent agreement stamp duty calculator](/tools/rent-agreement-stamp-duty-calculator-mumbai) does the sum.

**What changed in the Maharashtra rent rules in 2025?**
Less than the headlines suggest. The Maharashtra Rent Control (Amendment) Bill, 2025 dealt with structural alterations and the landlord's duty to keep a kitchen and essential services available. It did not cap deposits, change registration or adopt the Model Tenancy Act. Section 55, which requires registration, is unchanged.
