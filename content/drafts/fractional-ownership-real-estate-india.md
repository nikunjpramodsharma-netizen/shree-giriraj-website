## What is fractional ownership in real estate?

**Fractional ownership means several investors jointly own one income producing property, usually a leased office or warehouse, through a structure that manages it.** Each investor owns a fraction of a building none of them could buy alone.

Here is how it works. A leased office floor in a business park costs thirty crore rupees. A manager buys it through a trust and sells units to two hundred investors at fifteen lakh each. It collects the rent and passes it on after fees.

Until 2024 this ran on trust in the platform. The structure was usually a private company or a limited liability partnership. The platform chose the disclosure and the valuation, and selling meant finding another investor on the same platform.

SEBI approved a framework for small and medium REITs, called SM REITs, in November 2023. It notified the framework on 8 March 2024. **Fractional ownership of commercial property in India now has to sit inside a SEBI registered trust.**

---

## SM REIT rules that matter to you

**An SM REIT scheme holds 50 to 500 crore rupees of completed commercial property and asks at least 10 lakh rupees from each investor.** These are the rules under SEBI's March 2024 amendment.

- **Size:** each scheme holds assets worth 50 crore to 500 crore rupees. That is smaller than a full REIT and bigger than a single flat.
- **What it holds:** at least 95 percent in completed, revenue generating property. No land and no construction risk.
- **Minimum investment:** ten lakh rupees per investor. This is the deliberate line between an SM REIT and a mutual fund.
- **Who runs it:** an investment manager with a net worth of at least 20 crore rupees, a trustee, and SEBI registration for the trust.
- **Listing:** units are listed on a stock exchange, which gives fractional owners a public market to sell into.
- **Older platforms:** they had to apply to register within six months of the notification and bring their schemes inside the rules, or wind them down.

**Seven SM REITs were on SEBI's register when we read it on 16 November 2026:** Property Share Investment Trust (from August 2024), ImpactR, Emberstone, hBits, Amsa, Yield Foundry and TreBits (from October 2026).

Property Share is the one with listed schemes we could verify:

- PropShare Platina, a leased office in Bengaluru, on the BSE in December 2024 at about 353 crore rupees
- PropShare Titania, in Thane, in August 2025
- PropShare Celestia, in Ahmedabad, in April 2026

This is a young market, measured in a handful of listings, and it is best read that way.

## What you own in an SM REIT

**You own a unit in a scheme that owns one building or a few, with one or a few tenants on lease.** Your income is that rent, less the property's costs and the manager's fee. Your capital moves with the building's independent valuation and with the price the units trade at.

That is more concentrated than a full REIT, which holds dozens of buildings. It is also more knowable. You can read the lease, name the tenant and look at the building.

**Can you buy a fraction of a flat?** Not through this framework, which covers commercial property. Platforms selling shares in holiday homes or residential flats sit outside it. Ask them three questions: who holds the title, how do you exit, and who decides the valuation. Co owning a flat with family is a private arrangement, and it needs a written agreement on those same three questions.

## What is a REIT?

**A real estate investment trust, or REIT, is a SEBI registered trust that owns completed commercial property and pays out most of its rent.** Its units trade like shares.

India's rules are the SEBI (Real Estate Investment Trusts) Regulations 2014. They require:

- At least 80 percent of assets in completed, rent generating property
- At least 90 percent of distributable cash paid out to unit holders, at least twice a year
- A manager under a trustee, with independent valuations and quarterly disclosure

India has six listed REITs, all members of the Indian REITs Association:

- Embassy Office Parks, April 2019
- Mindspace Business Parks, 2020
- Brookfield India Real Estate Trust, 2021
- Nexus Select Trust, the first retail REIT, with malls, 2023
- Knowledge Realty Trust, August 2025
- Bagmane Prime Office REIT, May 2026

In the year to March 2026 they paid out more than 8,900 crore rupees, on the association's figures reported in May 2026. The association counted more than 5.2 lakh unit holders on results to the September 2026 quarter.

One of them owns a building you may know. Paradigm Mindspace Malad, on the Goregaon Malad Link Road, belongs to Mindspace REIT. It was 100 percent committed at 30 September 2026.

## How to invest in a REIT in India

1. **Open a demat and trading account** with any broker, if you do not have one.
2. **Search the REIT's name** as you would a share. Each listed REIT trades on the NSE and BSE.
3. **Buy from one unit.** SEBI's amendment, in force from 30 July 2021, cut the trading lot from a hundred units to one. It also cut the minimum subscription in a new offer from 50,000 rupees to between 10,000 and 15,000.
4. **Receive distributions** in your bank account, usually every quarter.
5. **Read the quarterly disclosures.** Occupancy, rent per square foot, lease expiries and debt are all published.

Speak to your chartered accountant about how distributions are taxed in your hands.

## Where a REIT or SM REIT beats a flat

- **Liquidity.** You can sell a REIT unit in a minute at a visible price. A flat in Borivali takes months and costs brokerage.
- **The ticket.** One unit against one flat. No flat in this belt lets you put in a lakh.
- **Diversification.** One REIT holds dozens of buildings and hundreds of tenants. One flat holds one tenant.
- **No management.** Nobody calls you about a leaking tap.
- **Commercial tenants.** Companies on long leases, with rent increases written in.
- **Transparency.** Valuations, occupancy and debt are disclosed and audited every quarter.

---

## Where a flat beats a REIT

- **Leverage.** A bank lends most of a flat's price over twenty years at home loan rates. Nobody lends on those terms to buy REIT units. That leverage is the main reason families here choose a flat.
- **Control.** You choose the flat, the floor, the tenant, the rent and the time to sell.
- **The chance to add value.** A tired 2 BHK in an old society, bought well, fitted out and let to the right tenant, can be worth more than you paid.
- **You can live in it.** Your parents can, or your child in ten years.
- **Redevelopment.** A flat in a society that redevelops can come back larger and newer, on the terms the society agrees. Read [redevelopment in Borivali and Kandivali](/blog/redevelopment-borivali-kandivali).
- **Slower price moves.** REIT units move with the stock market every day. A flat's value moves slowly and privately.

## How do REIT and flat income compare?

**A REIT's distribution yield and a flat's rental yield are different kinds of number.** A REIT yield is on commercial property, after professional management, on a price that moves daily. A September 2025 paper by Anarock and CREDAI put average REIT distribution yields at 6 to 7.5 percent at that time. That is a report, not a promise.

A flat's gross yield in this belt is modest, because people buy here to live. Square Yards reported 2.97 percent for Borivali West in September 2026 and 3.81 percent for Kandivali West in October 2026. Net is lower after society charges, tax and vacancy. But the flat's yield sits on a price you can borrow against, in an asset you can also live in.

## Against buying a shop or office yourself

**A shop you own gives you control and a loan; an SM REIT unit gives you a listed exit and no management.** A shop in Borivali West is yours. You choose the tenant and the rent, and you can borrow against it. It asks for a full price, your own management, and a narrower pool of buyers when you sell. An SM REIT unit asks for ten lakh and charges a fee in return.

## What to check before you buy an SM REIT

1. **The registration.** The trust and the scheme should be on SEBI's register and the units listed. If either is missing, it is not an SM REIT, whatever it is called.
2. **The tenant and the lease.** Who pays, for how long, with what increase, and when does the lease end?
3. **The building.** Where is it, and what is the vacancy nearby?
4. **The fees.** Management and performance fees come out of the rent before it reaches you.
5. **The exit.** Listing gives you a market, not a buyer at your price. Look at how the units have traded.

## Who should do what

**Lean towards a REIT if** the money is small or you may need it within a few years. It also suits you if you do not want to be a landlord.

**Lean towards an SM REIT if** you want one named commercial building and can lock up ten lakh rupees. You should be comfortable with a thin market.

**Lean towards a flat if** you can borrow well and can hold for ten years. A flat also suits you if you want something a child can live in or inherit.

**Do more than one if** you have the means. A common shape is a home or a let flat in a pocket you know, with REIT units for the money that must stay liquid.

## Common questions

**What is fractional ownership in real estate?**
Fractional ownership is shared ownership of one income producing property by many investors. In India, since SEBI's March 2024 framework, commercial fractional schemes must sit inside a registered SM REIT. Each investor holds units, receives a share of the rent and can sell on the stock exchange.

**Is fractional ownership of property safe in India?**
It is safer than it was. Inside the SM REIT framework there is a registered trust, a trustee and a manager with a minimum net worth. There is also independent valuation, disclosure and a listed exit. Income still depends on the tenant and value on the market.

**What is an SM REIT?**
An SM REIT is a small and medium real estate investment trust, regulated by SEBI since March 2024. A scheme holds 50 to 500 crore rupees of completed, rent producing commercial property. It sells units from ten lakh rupees and lists them on the exchange.

**How many SM REITs are there in India?**
SEBI's register listed seven SM REITs on 16 November 2026. They are Property Share, ImpactR, Emberstone, hBits, Amsa, Yield Foundry and TreBits. Property Share's three schemes are the listings we could verify.

**How do I invest in a REIT in India?**
You invest in a REIT through a demat account, the way you buy a share. Search the REIT's name on the NSE or BSE and buy from one unit. Distributions arrive in your bank account. Read the quarterly disclosures before and after.

**What is the minimum investment in a REIT?**
The minimum is one unit on the exchange, since SEBI's amendment of July 2021 cut the lot from a hundred units to one. In a new offer the minimum subscription is 10,000 to 15,000 rupees.

**Is a REIT better than buying a flat?**
A REIT is better at liquidity, small tickets, diversification and not being a landlord. A flat is better at leverage, control and adding value, and you can live in it. For many families here the answer is some of each.

**How is an SM REIT different from a normal REIT?**
The differences are size, concentration and ticket. A REIT holds many buildings and sells from one unit. An SM REIT holds one or a few and asks for ten lakh. The REIT is liquid and diversified, and the SM REIT is specific and, so far, thinly traded.

**Can I buy a fraction of a flat?**
Not under SM REIT rules, which cover commercial property. Residential fractional schemes sit outside them, so ask who holds the title, how you exit and who sets the valuation.

**Can I lose money in a REIT?**
Yes. Units trade on the stock exchange and move with it, and distributions depend on occupancy and rent. A REIT is regulated and transparent, not guaranteed.
