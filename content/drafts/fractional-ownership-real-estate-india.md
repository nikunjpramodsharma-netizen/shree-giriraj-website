## What a REIT is

A real estate investment trust, or REIT, is a trust registered with SEBI. It owns completed commercial property, collects the rent and distributes it. Its units are listed on the stock exchange and trade like shares.

India's rules are the SEBI (Real Estate Investment Trusts) Regulations of 2014. They require:

- At least 80 percent of assets in completed, rent generating property
- At least 90 percent of distributable cash paid out to unit holders, at least twice a year
- A manager under a trustee, with independent valuations and quarterly disclosure

You own no building. You own a slice of a portfolio that someone else runs, with a regulator watching.

India has six listed REITs:

- Embassy Office Parks, April 2019
- Mindspace Business Parks, 2020
- Brookfield India Real Estate Trust, 2021
- Nexus Select Trust, the first retail REIT, with malls, 2023
- Knowledge Realty Trust, August 2025
- Bagmane Prime Office REIT, May 2026

All six belong to the Indian REITs Association. In the year to March 2026 they paid out more than 8,900 crore rupees to about 4.25 lakh unit holders, on the association's figures.

One of them owns a building you may know. Paradigm Mindspace Malad, on the Goregaon Malad Link Road, belongs to Mindspace REIT. You can own a share of the office park that supplies Malad's tenants without owning a flat in Malad.

## How to invest in a REIT in India

1. **Open a demat and trading account** with any broker, if you do not have one.
2. **Search the REIT's name** as you would a share. Each listed REIT trades on the NSE and BSE.
3. **Buy from one unit.** SEBI's amendment, in force from 30 July 2021, cut the trading lot from a hundred units to one. It cut the minimum subscription in a new offer from 50,000 rupees to between 10,000 and 15,000.
4. **Receive distributions** in your bank account, usually every quarter.
5. **Read the quarterly disclosures.** Occupancy, rent per square foot, lease expiries and debt are all published.

A REIT distribution is not one thing. Embassy's investor page classes it as dividend, interest, repayment of debt from the trust's property companies, other income, or a mix. Each is taxed differently in your hands, and the mix changes by trust and by year. Your chartered accountant can tell you what that means for you.

## Fractional ownership before and after 2024

Fractional ownership means several investors jointly own one income producing property, usually a leased office or warehouse, through a platform that manages it.

The idea is simple. A leased office floor in a business park costs thirty crore rupees. A platform buys it through a company and sells shares to two hundred investors at fifteen lakh each. It collects the rent and passes it on after fees. Each investor owns a fraction of a building none of them could buy alone.

Until 2024 this ran on trust in the platform. The structure was usually a private company or a limited liability partnership. The platform chose the disclosure and the valuation. Selling meant finding another investor through the same platform. Some platforms were excellent, but the arrangement had no regulator.

SEBI approved a framework for small and medium REITs, called SM REITs, in November 2023. It notified the framework on 8 March 2024. Fractional ownership of commercial property now has to sit inside a registered trust.

---

## SM REIT rules that matter to you

- **Size:** each scheme holds assets worth 50 crore to 500 crore rupees. That is smaller than a full REIT and bigger than a single flat.
- **What it holds:** at least 95 percent in completed, revenue generating property. No land and no construction risk.
- **Minimum investment:** ten lakh rupees per investor. This is the deliberate line between an SM REIT and a mutual fund.
- **Who runs it:** an investment manager with a net worth of at least 20 crore rupees, a trustee, and SEBI registration for the trust.
- **Listing:** units are listed on a stock exchange. For the first time, fractional owners have a public market to sell into.
- **Older platforms:** they had to apply to register within six months of the notification and bring their schemes inside the rules, or wind them down.

Six SM REITs are on SEBI's register as of September 2026: Property Share Investment Trust (registered August 2024), ImpactR, Emberstone, hBits, Amsa and Yield Foundry.

Only Property Share has listed schemes so far:

- PropShare Platina, a leased office in Bengaluru, on the BSE in December 2024 at about 353 crore rupees
- PropShare Titania, in Thane, in August 2025
- PropShare Celestia, in Ahmedabad, in April 2026

A fourth scheme has been filed. This is a young market, measured in three listings, and it should be read that way.

## What you own in an SM REIT

A unit in a scheme that owns one building or a few, with one or a few tenants on lease. Your income is that rent, less the property's costs and the manager's fee. Your capital moves with the building's independent valuation and with the price at which the units trade.

That is more concentrated than a full REIT, which holds dozens of buildings. It is also more knowable. You can read the lease, name the tenant and look at the building.

Can you buy a fraction of a flat? Not through this framework, which covers commercial property. Platforms selling shares in holiday homes or residential flats sit outside it. Ask them three questions: who holds the title, how do you exit, and who decides the valuation. Co owning a flat with family is a private arrangement. It needs a written agreement covering those same three questions before the token is paid.

---

## Where a REIT beats a flat

- **Liquidity.** You can sell a REIT unit in a minute at a visible price. A flat in Borivali takes months and costs brokerage.
- **The ticket.** One unit against one flat. No flat in this belt lets you put in a lakh.
- **Diversification.** One REIT holds dozens of buildings and hundreds of tenants. One flat holds one tenant.
- **No management.** Nobody calls you about a leaking tap.
- **Commercial tenants.** Companies on long leases, with rent increases written in.
- **Transparency.** Valuations, occupancy and debt are disclosed and audited every quarter.

---

## Where a flat beats a REIT

- **Leverage.** A bank lends most of a flat's price over twenty years at home loan rates. Nobody lends on those terms to buy REIT units. That is why a flat bought with a loan and paid down by a tenant has built wealth for so many families here.
- **Control.** You choose the flat, the floor, the tenant, the rent and the time to sell.
- **The chance to add value.** A tired 2 BHK in an old society, bought well, fitted out and let to the right tenant, can be worth more than you paid.
- **You can live in it.** Your parents can, or your child in ten years.
- **Redevelopment.** A flat in a society that redevelops comes back larger and newer, at no cost to you.
- **Slower price moves.** REIT units move with the stock market every day. A flat's value moves slowly and privately.

## The income comparison, honestly

A REIT's distribution yield and a flat's rental yield are different kinds of number.

A REIT yield is on commercial property, after professional management and before your tax, on a price that moves daily. A September 2025 paper by Anarock and CREDAI put average REIT distribution yields at 6 to 7.5 percent at that time. That is a report, not a promise.

A flat's gross yield in this belt is modest, because people buy here to live. Square Yards reported about 3.9 percent for Kandivali West and 2.65 percent for Borivali West in mid 2026. Net is lower after society charges, tax and vacancy. But the flat's yield sits on a price you may have borrowed against, in an asset you can also live in.

The [rental yield article](/blog/rental-yield-mumbai) shows the gross and net sums for a flat. We can put the two side by side for you. On one side is your flat, with its real rent and outgoings. On the other is the REIT, with its last four quarters of disclosures.

## Against buying a shop or office yourself

A shop in Borivali West is yours. You choose the tenant and the rent, and you can borrow against it. It asks for a full price, your own management, and a small pool of buyers when you sell. An SM REIT unit asks for ten lakh, no management and a listed exit. In return you give up control and pay a fee. Our [commercial property article](/blog/commercial-property-investment-mumbai) covers the direct route.

## What to check before you buy an SM REIT

1. **The registration.** The trust and the scheme should be on SEBI's register and the units listed. If either is missing, it is not an SM REIT, whatever it is called.
2. **The tenant and the lease.** Who pays, for how long, with what increase, and when does the lease end?
3. **The building.** Where is it, and what is the vacancy nearby?
4. **The fees.** Management and performance fees come out of the rent before it reaches you.
5. **The exit.** Listing gives you a market, not a buyer at your price. Look at how the units have traded.
6. **The tax.** Distributions are taxed by component, and selling units has its own treatment. Ask your chartered accountant.

## Who should do what

**Lean towards a REIT if** the money is small or you may need it within a few years. It also suits you if you do not want to be a landlord.

**Lean towards an SM REIT if** you want one named commercial building and can lock up ten lakh rupees. You should be comfortable with a thin market.

**Lean towards a flat if** you can borrow well and can hold for ten years. A flat also suits you if you want something a child can live in or inherit.

**Do more than one if** you have the means. Most of our investing clients end up with this shape. They own a home or a let flat in a pocket they know. They hold REIT units for the money that must stay liquid.

## Common questions

**How do I invest in a REIT in India?**
Through a demat account, the way you buy a share. Search the REIT's name on the NSE or BSE and buy from one unit. Distributions arrive in your bank account. Read the quarterly disclosures before and after.

**What is the minimum investment in a REIT?**
One unit on the exchange, since SEBI's amendment of July 2021 cut the lot from a hundred units to one. In a new offer the minimum subscription is 10,000 to 15,000 rupees. Under the original 2014 rules the lot was a hundred units and the minimum subscription 50,000 rupees.

**Is a REIT better than buying a flat?**
It is better at liquidity, small tickets, diversification and not being a landlord. It is weaker on leverage, control, adding value and living in it. For most families here the answer is some of each.

**What is an SM REIT?**
A small and medium real estate investment trust, regulated by SEBI since March 2024. A scheme holds 50 to 500 crore rupees of completed, rent producing commercial property. It sells units from ten lakh rupees, lists them on the exchange and distributes the rent.

**Is fractional ownership of property safe in India?**
Safer than it was. Inside the SM REIT framework there is a registered trust and a trustee. The manager must meet a minimum net worth. There is independent valuation, disclosure and a listed exit. Outside it, on unregulated platforms, none of that is required. Regulated is not guaranteed: income depends on the tenant and value on the market.

**Can I buy a fraction of a flat?**
Not under SM REIT rules, which cover commercial property. Residential fractional schemes sit outside them and carry open questions on title, exit and valuation.

**How is an SM REIT different from a normal REIT?**
Size, concentration and ticket. A REIT holds many buildings and sells units from one unit. An SM REIT holds one or a few and asks for ten lakh. The REIT is liquid and diversified. The SM REIT is specific and, so far, thinly traded.

**Are REIT distributions taxed?**
Yes, and differently for each part: interest, dividend and repayment of capital are treated separately. Ask your chartered accountant what the mix means for you.

**Can I lose money in a REIT?**
Yes. Units trade on the stock exchange and move with it, and distributions depend on occupancy and rent. A REIT is regulated and transparent, not guaranteed.
