## The short answer

Conveyance transfers ownership of the land and building from the developer to the housing society. Under the MOFA rules the promoter should convey **within four months of the society being registered**. Where that does not happen, the society can apply to the **District Deputy Registrar** as competent authority for a unilateral **deemed conveyance**, without the developer's signature.

---

## What owning a flat does not give you

You own your flat. The society, collectively, is supposed to own the land beneath it. Until conveyance happens, the developer still does.

Most owners discover this only when the society tries to redevelop and finds that it cannot, because it does not own the thing it is proposing to redevelop.

---

## The four month rule almost nobody enforces

Where no period is agreed, the MOFA rules require the promoter to convey within **four months of the society's registration**. In practice a great many Mumbai societies are decades past that and have never pushed.

That is what deemed conveyance exists for.

---

## Deemed conveyance, in outline

**Who decides.** The District Deputy Registrar acts as the competent authority.

**Who applies.** The society, or its members, apply in writing.

**What goes in.** The application is made in the prescribed **Form VII** under the MOFA rules, with a court fee stamp of **2,000 rupees**, enclosing true copies of the registered agreement for sale of every member, the society's registration certificate, and the occupation certificate where there is one.

**How long it takes.** Proceedings before the competent authority are to be completed **within six months**. Treat that as the design rather than a promise, and budget for gathering the member agreements taking longer than the hearing does.

**What comes out.** An order that transfers title to the society without the developer signing anything, which is the entire point of the mechanism.

Much of the process can now be done online, provided the society actually holds its documents.

## If your building is talking about redevelopment

**Ask about conveyance first, not last.** A society that does not own its land cannot redevelop on its own terms, and every other conversation about builders and terms is premature until that is settled.

---

## Common questions

**What happens if a society never gets conveyance?**
It stays dependent on the developer for anything requiring the landowner's consent, and it cannot redevelop on its own terms. The flats remain owned by their members throughout, but the collective asset stays out of reach.

**Who is the competent authority for deemed conveyance?**
The District Deputy Registrar.

**How long does deemed conveyance take?**
The proceedings are to be completed within six months. Assembling every member's registered agreement usually takes longer than the proceedings.

**Does conveyance affect redevelopment?**
Materially. It is the difference between a society negotiating from ownership and one negotiating from dependence.

---

## If you are buying in an older building

Ask whether conveyance is done. Almost no buyer asks, and the answer tells you a great deal about how well the society is run.
