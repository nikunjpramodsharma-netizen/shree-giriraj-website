## The short answer

Conveyance transfers ownership of the land and building from the developer to the housing society. Under MOFA, Maharashtra's flat ownership law, the developer should convey **within four months of the society being registered**. If that does not happen, the society can apply to the **District Deputy Registrar** for **deemed conveyance**, which transfers the land without the developer's signature.

---

## What does owning a flat give you, and what does it not?

You own your flat. The society, as a group, is meant to own the land beneath it. Until conveyance happens, the developer still does.

**Conveyance** simply means the legal transfer of the land and building into the society's name. **Deemed conveyance** is the same transfer, ordered by a government officer when the developer will not sign.

Most owners learn this when the society plans redevelopment. That is the moment they find the society does not yet own what it wants to rebuild.

## When should the developer convey the land?

**MOFA** is the Maharashtra Ownership Flats Act, 1963. It is the state law that governs how builders sell flats.

Where no other period is agreed, its rules require the developer to convey **within four months of the society's registration**. Many Mumbai societies are decades past that date and have never asked.

Deemed conveyance exists for exactly these societies.

---

## How does deemed conveyance work?

| Step | What it involves |
|---|---|
| Who decides | The District Deputy Registrar, acting as the competent authority (the officer the law empowers to decide) |
| Who applies | The society, or its members, in writing |
| The form | **Form VII** under the MOFA rules |
| The fee | A court fee stamp of **2,000 rupees** |
| How long | The proceedings are to be completed **within six months** |
| The result | An order that transfers title to the society, with no signature needed from the developer |

The application encloses true copies of:

- the registered agreement for sale of every member
- the society's registration certificate
- the occupation certificate, where there is one

Treat six months as the design of the process. Gathering every member's agreement usually takes longer than the hearing itself, so we start there.

Much of the process can now be done online, once the society holds its documents.

---

## Why do developers delay conveyance?

The law gives a developer four months from the society's registration to convey the land and building. That is **section 11** of MOFA, read with **rule 9** of the 1964 rules. The delay is rarely about paperwork.

The usual reason is development potential. Two terms matter here:

- **FSI**, or floor space index, is how much floor area the rules allow on a plot.
- **TDR**, or transferable development rights, is extra building rights bought from elsewhere and used on the plot.

Until the land is conveyed, any unused FSI and any TDR loaded onto it stay with the developer. Some agreements hold this open with a clause that postpones conveyance until a future phase is complete.

Other reasons are unsold flats, disputed dues and plain inertia. None of them is a defence in law.

---

## What have the courts said?

In February 2026 the Bombay High Court dealt with exactly that postponing clause and refused to enforce it.

Justice Amit Borkar explained why. If such clauses were honoured, a promoter could keep delaying conveyance. In his words, it could be done "by referring to some future plan, additional FSI, or a proposed phase". He also held that a contractual promise is not a licence to withhold conveyance at will.

The remedy is deemed conveyance through the District Deputy Registrar of Co operative Societies. That officer can order the sub registrar to register the conveyance without the developer's signature.

**From 31 December 2025 that route is open to RERA registered projects too**, under a new section 11A added to MOFA.

## How many societies still lack conveyance?

Nobody has an official count. A figure of 85 percent for Mumbai was cited at a seminar in 2014. It is old, and no official count exists for Borivali, Kandivali or Malad.

So on any older building, the simple habit is to ask rather than assume.

## Common questions

**What happens if a society never gets conveyance?**
It stays dependent on the developer for anything that needs the landowner's consent, and it cannot redevelop on its own terms. The members still own their flats throughout. The shared asset, the land, stays out of reach.

**Who is the competent authority for deemed conveyance?**
The District Deputy Registrar of Co operative Societies for the district, appointed under the Maharashtra Ownership Flats Act. The society applies there and the promoter is given a hearing. The registrar has to decide within six months. That office's certificate lets the sub registrar register the conveyance without the developer's signature.

**How long does deemed conveyance take?**
The proceedings are to be completed within six months. Collecting every member's registered agreement usually takes longer than the proceedings.

**Does conveyance affect redevelopment?**
Yes, a great deal. It is the difference between a society negotiating as the owner and one negotiating as a dependant.

**Can a RERA registered project use deemed conveyance?**
Yes. From 31 December 2025 the route is open to RERA registered projects too, under a new section 11A added to the Maharashtra Ownership Flats Act.

**What does a society need to apply?**
Form VII under the MOFA rules, with a court fee stamp of 2,000 rupees. Enclose true copies of every member's registered agreement for sale and the society's registration certificate. Add the occupation certificate where there is one.
