## The short answer

A sale deed is the document that transfers ownership of a property. It records the parties, the property, the price, the fact that payment was made, and the transfer of title and possession. **Without a duly stamped and registered deed of conveyance, no right or interest in immovable property passes at all**, however much has been paid.

---

## The clauses that matter

**Parties.** Full names, ages, addresses. Check every spelling against PAN and Aadhaar. A mismatch surfaces years later at the worst possible moment, usually when you are the one selling.

**Property description.** Survey or CTS number, area, boundaries, floor, building. **This is what identifies the flat legally**, not the flat number on the door.

**Consideration.** The price, and confirmation that it has been received. Where a home loan is involved the disbursement is normally recorded as part of the consideration, so the deed shows what came from you and what came from the lender.

**Title covenant.** The seller's statement that they have the right to sell and that the property is free of encumbrances. **This is the clause you fall back on if something turns out to be wrong**, so read it rather than skim it.

**Possession.** When it passes. It is not always the same day as registration, and the gap is where disputes live.

**Indemnity.** What the seller owes you if their statements prove untrue.

---

## Sale deed versus agreement to sale

They get used interchangeably and they do different things.

An **agreement to sale** is a promise to transfer on agreed terms at a future point. It creates obligations between the parties. It does not move ownership.

A **sale deed** is the transfer itself. Ownership passes on a duly stamped and registered deed, and not before.

The practical consequence: money paid under an agreement to sale buys you a contractual right against the seller, not the flat. That is a meaningful difference if the seller changes their mind.

---

## Registration is not optional

For immovable property above the statutory value, a sale deed must be registered. An unregistered deed does not transfer title, and the courts are consistent about this. It is why the registration step is not administrative box ticking.

Registration also produces your [Index 2](/blog/index-2-property-document), which is the one page proof that it happened.

---

## Fixing a mistake

Errors happen, most often in names, areas and boundaries. The route is a **rectification deed**, executed between the same parties, correcting the error and registered in the same way.

It is straightforward while both parties are cooperative and available. It becomes considerably harder when the seller has moved, lost interest or died, which is why the time to read the description carefully is before signing rather than after.

## Common questions

**Does a sale deed need to be registered?**
Yes, for immovable property above the statutory value. Without registration no title passes, whatever the deed says and whatever has been paid.

**What is the difference between a sale deed and an agreement to sale?**
The agreement is a promise to transfer on agreed terms. The deed is the transfer. Ownership moves on the registered deed.

**What if there is a mistake in the sale deed?**
A rectification deed between the same parties, registered in the same way. Easy while everyone is available and cooperative, difficult afterwards.

**Is mutation enough to prove I own it?**
No. Mutation updates municipal records for tax. The deed is what gives you ownership. See [mutation of property](/blog/mutation-of-property).

---

## Before you sign

Read the property description and the title covenant yourself, out loud if it helps. Those two clauses are the ones you will care about if anything ever goes wrong.
