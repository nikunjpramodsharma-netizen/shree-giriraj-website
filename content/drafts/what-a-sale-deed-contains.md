## The short answer

A sale deed is the document that transfers ownership of a property from seller to buyer. It records the parties, the property, the consideration, the fact that payment has been made, and the transfer of title and possession. **It becomes effective on registration**, and it is the document you will be asked for every time you deal with the property afterwards.

---

## The clauses that matter

**Parties.** Full names, ages, addresses. Check the spelling against PAN and Aadhaar, because a mismatch surfaces years later at the worst moment.

**Property description.** Survey or CTS number, area, boundaries, floor, building. This is what identifies the flat legally, not the flat number on the door.

**Consideration.** The price, and confirmation it has been received. [ VERIFY: how part payment and loan disbursement are usually recorded. ]

**Title covenant.** The seller's statement that they have the right to sell and that the property is free of encumbrances. This is the clause you rely on if something is wrong later.

**Possession.** When it passes. It is not always the same day as registration.

**Indemnity.** What the seller owes you if their statements turn out to be untrue.
