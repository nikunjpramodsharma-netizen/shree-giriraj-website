## The short answer

A sale deed is the document that transfers ownership of a property. It records the parties, the property, the price, the fact that payment was made, and the handover of title and possession. **Ownership passes only once the deed is duly stamped and registered**, however much has already been paid.

---

## Which clauses in a sale deed matter most?

| Clause | What it says | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| **Parties** | Full names, ages and addresses of buyer and seller | Every spelling matches PAN and Aadhaar |
| **Property description** | Survey or CTS number, area, boundaries, floor and building | It matches your share certificate. **This identifies the flat legally**, not the number on the door |
| **Consideration** | The price, and confirmation it has been received | Any home loan payment is shown, so the deed records what came from you and what came from the lender |
| **Title covenant** | The seller's promise that they have the right to sell, free of any claims | Read it in full. **This is the clause you rely on later** |
| **Possession** | When the flat is handed over | The date is clear, even if it differs from the registration day |
| **Indemnity** | What the seller owes you if their statements prove untrue | It is specific, not a one line catch all |

A few of these words in plain terms:

- **Consideration** is the legal word for the price paid.
- A **covenant** is a formal promise written into the deed.
- **Indemnity** is the seller's promise to make good your loss if their statements prove untrue.
- An **encumbrance** is any claim on the property, such as a mortgage or a court case.

## How is a sale deed different from an agreement to sale?

People use the two names as if they meant the same thing. They do different jobs.

An **agreement to sale** is a promise to transfer on agreed terms at a future date. It creates obligations between buyer and seller. It does not move ownership.

A **sale deed** is the transfer itself. Ownership passes on a duly stamped and registered deed, and not before.

In practice, money paid under an agreement to sale buys you a contractual right against the seller. The registered deed is what makes the flat yours.

## Does a sale deed have to be registered?

Yes. A sale deed for immovable property above the statutory value must be registered. Section 17 of the Registration Act, 1908 sets that value at 100 rupees, so it covers any flat sale.

An unregistered deed does not transfer title, and the courts are consistent about this. That is why registration is a real legal step, not a formality.

Registration also produces your [Index 2](/blog/index-2-property-document). That is the one page proof that the registration happened.

## How do you correct a mistake after signing?

Errors happen, most often in names, areas and boundaries. The fix is a **rectification deed**. It is a short deed between the same parties that corrects the error, registered in the same way.

It is straightforward while both parties are cooperative and available. It takes longer once the seller has moved, lost interest or died. So the best time to read the description is before signing.

## What we check before a client signs

We check five things, in this order.

1. **The schedule.** This is the part of the deed that describes the flat. Flat number, floor, wing, carpet area, parking and the building's boundaries must match the share certificate and the seller's own purchase deed. An error here carries into every later document, so we fix it first.
2. **The recitals.** These are the opening paragraphs that say how the seller came to own the flat. They must match the chain of title in the search report, name for name and document for document. If a recital skips a link, or names a different buyer from the earlier deed, we get that answered first.
3. **The encumbrance declaration and the indemnity, together.** The seller should state that there is no mortgage, no litigation, no tenant and no claim from a family member. The seller should also agree to make good any loss if that proves untrue. A specific declaration protects you far better than a one line, all purpose one.
4. **Possession.** The date, and the condition the flat is handed over in.
5. **The dues clause.** It should put property tax, society charges, electricity and water up to the possession date on the seller.

A deed that is right on these five is a deed you can sign with confidence.

## Common questions

**Does a sale deed need to be registered?**
Yes, for immovable property above the statutory value. Without registration no title passes, whatever the deed says and whatever has been paid.

**What is the difference between a sale deed and an agreement to sale?**
The agreement is a promise to transfer on agreed terms. The deed is the transfer. Ownership moves on the registered deed.

**What if there is a mistake in the sale deed?**
The fix is a rectification deed between the same parties, registered in the same way. It is easy while everyone is available and cooperative, and harder afterwards.

**Is mutation enough to prove I own it?**
No. Mutation updates the municipal records for tax. The deed is what gives you ownership. See [mutation of property](/blog/mutation-of-property).

**Is a sale deed valid if it is not registered?**
No. Under sections 17 and 49 of the Registration Act, 1908, a deed transferring immovable property must be registered to pass title. An unregistered one cannot be used to prove the transfer. Signing and paying are not enough; registration at the sub registrar's office is what makes the ownership yours.

**What is the difference between a sale deed and a title deed?**
Title deed is the general name for any document that proves ownership. A sale deed is one kind of title deed, the kind created by a purchase. A gift deed, a partition deed or a court decree can also be a title deed. So every registered sale deed is a title deed, but not every title deed is a sale deed.
