## The short answer

Yes. A MHADA flat can be let out once the Mumbai Board issues a no objection certificate (NOC) for rent. The Board's notified time limit is 8 days, and the Estate Manager decides. **After the NOC, you register the leave and licence agreement like any other. Apply on the Aaple Sarkar portal.**

---

## How does the NOC for rent work, step by step?

Six steps take you from a MHADA flat to a registered tenancy. The Board has 8 days to decide once your application is complete.

| Step | What happens | Who | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Confirm the flat stands in your name on the Board's record | The allottee (the person MHADA allotted the flat to) | Before you apply |
| 2 | Gather the allotment letter, latest Board receipt, society NOC, identity proof and tenant details | The allottee | Before you apply |
| 3 | Apply for the NOC for rent on Aaple Sarkar or at the Estate Manager's office | The allottee | On the day you apply |
| 4 | The Board decides | The Estate Manager, the officer the law names to decide | 8 days from a complete application |
| 5 | Sign the leave and licence agreement, with a term inside the NOC period | Licensor (the owner) and licensee (the tenant) | After the NOC |
| 6 | Register the agreement | The licensor | Before the keys change hands |

The time limit comes from the Aaple Sarkar portal's own listing of the service.

## Why does a MHADA flat need permission to let?

A MHADA flat is held under an allotment. The allotment comes with the Estate Management, Sale, Transfer and Exchange of Tenements Regulations, 1981. A tenement is the flat or unit the Board has let to the allottee.

The Regulations keep the Board interested in the flat for as long as the tenancy runs. That is why a sale needs its permission, and why letting the flat to someone else does too.

In practice, the Board wants to confirm three things:

- The allottee is still the allottee.
- The flat is not being handed over under the name of a tenancy.
- The person moving in is a licensee, not a buyer in disguise.

The NOC is how the Board records all three.

## Is a MHADA NOC needed to rent out a flat?

Yes. On the Government of Maharashtra's Aaple Sarkar portal, it is listed as a no objection certificate for letting a flat, plot or commercial unit. The MHADA Mumbai Board delivers it.

It is a notified service under the Right to Public Services Act, 2015. That gives you real protections:

- **A time limit:** 8 days on the portal.
- **A designated officer:** the Estate Manager, who must decide your application.
- **A right of appeal:** to the Executive Engineer first, then the Chief Officer.

A refusal has to be justified, and a delay can be appealed.

## What does the application need?

The portal's page lists no separate document requirements, because the particulars are collected in the application itself. The Estate Manager's office wants to see two things. You are the allottee. The flat is the right flat. For what can be due on a MHADA flat and who bills it, see our guide to [MHADA dues](/blog/mhada-dues-lease-rent-property-tax).

Keep these ready:

- The allotment letter
- The latest rent or maintenance receipt from the Board
- The society's no objection, where a society exists
- Identity proof for the allottee
- The tenant's details

If the allottee has died and the tenancy has not yet moved to an heir, that transfer comes first. The Board issues the NOC only in the name of the person on its record. Our guide to [MHADA transfer on death](/blog/mhada-transfer-on-death) covers it.

The Board's exact document checklist and any fee for the NOC are not published on the Aaple Sarkar page. Confirm both at the Estate Manager's office before you submit. Treat any figure a third party quotes as unconfirmed until the Board's receipt says so.

## What comes after the NOC?

The NOC clears the flat to be let. It does not create the tenancy. The tenancy is the leave and licence agreement.

Under section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999, that agreement must be in writing and registered. The duty to register sits with the licensor. A registered agreement is the one a court will rely on for the terms.

Two points are worth writing into a MHADA leave and licence:

- **Say it is a licence.** The agreement should state that it does not transfer any interest in the tenement. The Regulations are about transfers, so this keeps the two apart.
- **Keep the term inside the NOC.** A licence that runs past its NOC has no permission for the extra months.

Ask the Estate Manager whether the NOC carries a fixed validity and whether a renewed licence needs a fresh one. MHADA runs a revalidation process for NOCs. The easy habit is to assume a renewal needs one.

## What does the NOC protect?

With the NOC, your tenancy sits on the Board's record and your allotment stays secure. A leave and licence over the flat is also one you can enforce if the licensee stops paying.

Without it, the occupation is unauthorised under the Regulations, and the Board has power to act. Some brokers say nobody checks. Societies, neighbours and the Board all can, and the Board does when a complaint lands. Eight days is a small price for a clear record.

## Common questions

**Is a MHADA NOC needed to rent out a flat?**
Yes. The NOC for rent is a notified Right to Public Services item delivered by the MHADA Mumbai Board. Apply through Aaple Sarkar or the Estate Manager's office.

**How long does the MHADA NOC for rent take?**
The notified time limit on the Aaple Sarkar portal is 8 days, with the Estate Manager as the designated officer. If it is delayed or refused without adequate reason, you can appeal to the Executive Engineer, then to the Chief Officer.

**What happens if I rent a MHADA flat without NOC?**
The occupation is unauthorised under the Regulations, which puts the allotment at risk. A leave and licence over the flat is also hard to enforce. The NOC is quicker than sorting that out later.

**Does the leave and licence still need registering?**
Yes. Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act applies to a MHADA flat as it does to any other. The NOC and the registered agreement are two separate things, and you need both.

**Can an heir rent out the flat before the transfer is done?**
Not in their own name. The Board issues the NOC to the allottee on its record. If the allottee has died, the transfer to the heir under the Regulations comes first.
