The price on the builder's sheet is not what you pay. On a new project in Mumbai there are three government charges and a list of builder charges on top. Knowing them early lets you budget for the whole amount from the start. This guide puts the whole list in one place, with the rates as they stand in November 2026, when each one is paid, and what happens to them if you sell before possession.

Rates change in the state budget, usually in March. Check the current figure before you sign; we do, for every booking.

## The three government charges

### 1 and 2. Stamp duty and registration

Same as on any flat: 6% stamp duty in Mumbai (5% for a woman buying alone), on the higher of the agreement value and the ready reckoner value, plus registration of 1% capped at ₹30,000. On a ₹2 Cr flat that is ₹12 lakh and ₹30,000. The rates, the woman buyer's concession, refunds and the reckoner rule are all in our [stamp duty and registration guide](/blog/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-mumbai), so this page does not repeat them.

What is different on an under construction flat is the timing: the duty is paid at the agreement for sale, which RERA requires before the builder takes more than 10% of the price. So it comes early, often within weeks of booking, not at possession.

### 3. GST

GST applies to a flat that is still under construction, because what you are buying is partly a service. The rates are 5% of the agreement value for a normal flat and 1% for an affordable flat (in a metro, a carpet area up to 60 sq m and a price up to ₹45 lakh). The builder cannot claim input credit under these rates, so there is nothing to pass back to you.

GST does not apply to a flat that has its occupation certificate, or to a resale. Our [under construction or ready to move guide](/blog/under-construction-vs-ready-to-move-flat) compares the two side by side. So the same flat is 5% dearer under construction than it will be after OC, which is one of the reasons the builder's rate rises at OC.

On a ₹2 Cr flat: ₹10 lakh.

TDS also applies when the price is ₹50 lakh or more. It is not an extra cost, and your chartered accountant will guide you on it.

## The builder's charges

These vary by project and should be on the builder's sheet as separate lines. Ask for all of them before you book, and ask which ones are included in the quoted rate:

- Floor rise, usually a fixed amount per sq ft per floor above a base floor
- Preferential location charge (PLC) for a sea view, garden view or corner
- Parking, often a fixed sum per slot
- Society formation and share money
- Legal and documentation charges
- Infrastructure or development charges
- Advance maintenance, usually 12 to 24 months collected at possession
- Club house or amenity charges, if the project has them

GST applies to most of these too. A sheet that shows the rate "plus charges" without listing them is a sheet to question.

## When each one is paid

| Charge | When |
|---|---|
| Stamp duty and registration | At the agreement for sale, which under RERA has to be registered before the builder takes more than 10% of the price. The duty is paid before or on the day of registration. |
| GST | With each instalment to the builder, on that instalment. |
| Builder charges | As the sheet says; some at booking, most at possession. |

## The total, on a ₹2 Cr flat

Stamp duty ₹12 lakh, registration ₹30,000, GST ₹10 lakh. About ₹22.3 lakh in government charges, or around 11% on top of the price, before any of the builder's own charges. Budget for 12 to 14% all in.

## If you sell before possession

Two things to know, because they decide whether an early sale makes sense.

**The buyer pays their own stamp duty.** The resale is a new registered agreement, so the new buyer pays duty again on the full price. That is why a buyer of an under construction unit expects the price to be a little below what the builder is asking for a fresh booking.

**Maharashtra gives a set off on a quick resale.** If the same property is sold again within one year of the first agreement, the duty on the second agreement is charged only on the increase in price, not the whole price, because the earlier duty is adjusted against it. This makes an exit inside a year much cheaper for your buyer than one after it. The exact conditions sit in the Maharashtra Stamp Act and the registrar's practice, so confirm them with a stamp duty consultant before you build a plan around it. We can introduce one.

**GST on the resale.** If you sell before OC, your buyer takes over the remaining instalments and pays GST on those. There is no GST on the amount you receive for your unit, since you are not the builder.

**Income tax.** A gain on the sale is taxed, and how long you held the flat changes how. Your chartered accountant will guide you on it before you sell.

## Common questions

**Is stamp duty refundable if the builder cancels?**
Yes. You can apply for a refund of the duty within the time limit set by the Act, with the cancellation deed. The registration fee is not refunded.

**Can I pay GST from a home loan?**
Most banks fund the agreement value, and some include GST in the loan amount for under construction flats. Ask the bank before you assume; stamp duty and registration are almost never funded.

**Do NRIs pay the same?**
Yes. Stamp duty, registration and GST are the same for an NRI buyer. [How an NRI books from abroad](/guides/nri-book-under-construction-flat-mumbai)
